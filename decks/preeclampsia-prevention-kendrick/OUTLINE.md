# Preeclampsia Prevention: A Shared Plan for Patients and Providers

OpenMFM Presentation Standard v1.0 | Approved by user; local production complete | September 30, 2026

## Educational design

**Central message:** Identify risk early, offer clinician-directed low-dose aspirin when appropriate, and make counseling and follow-up reliable. Prevention lowers risk; it does not eliminate preeclampsia.

**Audience:** People planning pregnancy or receiving prenatal care, their support people, and prenatal physicians and advanced practice providers. Begin with plain-language patient education; mark the transition to the provider toolkit explicitly.

**Source project:** Nadia Kendrick, MSN, FNP-BC, Doctor of Nursing Practice student, Georgia Southern University. Source: *Kendrick_Nadia_DNPProjectForms (1).pdf*, five pages, supplied by the user. Project title: *Strengthening Preeclampsia Prevention in At-Risk Pregnant Women Through a Standardized Low-Dose Aspirin Prophylaxis Protocol*. Setting: Atlanta Perinatal Associates.

**Prerequisites:** No clinical knowledge needed for the patient section. Define preeclampsia, placenta, prophylaxis, and risk factors before using them.

**Misconceptions to address:** Aspirin is appropriate for everyone; aspirin treats established preeclampsia; absence of symptoms excludes disease; prevention guarantees protection; a patient declining aspirin is automatically a protocol failure.

**Key decisions:** Who meets risk criteria? Is aspirin appropriate after review of contraindications? When should it start? What does the patient understand? How is the decision documented? Which symptoms require immediate assessment?

**Six-month memory:** Ask about personal risk early. Follow an individualized prevention plan. Seek care for warning signs during pregnancy and after birth.

## Editorial boundaries

- The supplied document describes a planned 12-week quality improvement initiative. It contains no completed study results, sample size, outcome estimates, or demonstrated reduction in preeclampsia.
- Provider recruitment, consent signatures, institutional procedures, and email instructions are source content, not authorization to recruit, contact anyone, or collect data through OpenMFM.
- Providers are the research participants described in the source. Patients receive clinical care and may benefit indirectly; do not imply that viewing this deck enrolls patients in research.
- Omit the uncited approximately 24% aspirin compliance figure and the worldwide mortality ranking. Their population, denominator, date, and supporting references are not supplied.
- Describe IRB approval only as a statement in the source if needed; do not imply independent verification or current recruitment availability. Omit personal contact information, signatures, and administrative consent language from the public deck.
- The source says "three" evaluation components but enumerates four. Present four documented domains: screening, counseling, aspirin decision, and gestational age.
- Preserve the project's four decision categories: initiated, continued, not indicated, declined. Explain that contraindications and individualized exceptions also need documentation; a clinically justified nonprescription is not automatically poor care.
- Clearly label external clinical guidance separately from the project workflow and proposed measures.
