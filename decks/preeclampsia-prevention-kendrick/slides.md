<div class="eyebrow">PATIENT EDUCATION · THE CONDITION</div>

# Preeclampsia affects blood pressure and organs

<p class="lead">Preeclampsia is a pregnancy condition involving high blood pressure and signs that organs may be affected.</p>
<div class="grid three"><div class="card"><h2>Blood pressure</h2><p>It may rise even when you feel well.</p></div><div class="card"><h2>Organs</h2><p>The kidneys, liver, brain, or lungs may be affected.</p></div><div class="card"><h2>Pregnancy</h2><p>It can affect the placenta, the organ that supports the baby's growth.</p></div></div>
<p class="takeaway">It usually starts after 20 weeks. It can also develop after birth.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: ACOG patient FAQ [5]; USPSTF evidence review [4].</p>

<div class="eyebrow">PATIENT EDUCATION · PLANNING AHEAD</div>

# Share your history before or early in pregnancy

<div class="flow"><div><b>BEFORE PREGNANCY</b><strong>Review your health</strong><p>Discuss prior pregnancy problems and long-term conditions.</p></div><div><b>FIRST PRENATAL VISIT</b><strong>Ask about your risk</strong><p>Bring a medication list and tell your team about allergies.</p></div><div><b>EARLY PREGNANCY</b><strong>Write down the plan</strong><p>Ask whether aspirin is appropriate and when to start.</p></div></div>
<p class="takeaway">Prevention starts with a clinical conversation. Do not start aspirin on your own.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: ACOG patient FAQ [5]; USPSTF recommendation [3].</p>

---
<div class="eyebrow">PATIENT EDUCATION · RISK SCREENING</div>

# Risk screening asks about more than today's symptoms

<div class="grid two"><div class="card"><h2>Medical and pregnancy history</h2><p>Prior preeclampsia, high blood pressure before pregnancy, diabetes, kidney disease, autoimmune illness, or twins may matter.</p></div><div class="card"><h2>Other parts of your story</h2><p>Your first pregnancy, age, family history, fertility treatment, and access to care also help your clinician assess risk.</p></div></div>
<p class="takeaway">On the chart form, choose “not sure” when you do not know. Your clinician will review the answers.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: ACOG/SMFM advisory [1]; USPSTF [3]. Form workflow: local adaptation of project [8].</p>

---
<div class="eyebrow">PATIENT EDUCATION · PREVENTION</div>

# Low-dose aspirin can lower risk for eligible patients

<div class="grid two"><div class="card emphasis"><span class="label">PREVENTIVE TREATMENT</span><h2>Lower the chance</h2><p>A small daily dose can reduce the chance of preeclampsia in people at increased risk.</p></div><div class="card"><span class="label">CONTINUED CARE</span><h2>Keep your checks</h2><p>Aspirin does not eliminate risk or treat preeclampsia that has already developed.</p></div></div>
<p class="takeaway">“Prophylaxis” means treatment used to help prevent a condition.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: USPSTF recommendation [3] and randomized-trial evidence review [4].</p>

---
<div class="eyebrow">PATIENT EDUCATION · TIMING</div>

# Agree on the dose and start date with your clinician

<div class="dose"><strong>81 mg</strong><span>daily is the US guideline dose<br>for eligible patients</span></div>
<div class="flow"><div><b>12 WEEKS</b><strong>Start window opens</strong></div><div><b>BEFORE 16 WEEKS</b><strong>Preferred timing</strong></div><div><b>28 WEEKS</b><strong>End of recommended start window</strong></div></div>
<p class="takeaway">Guidance continues daily aspirin until delivery. Follow your clinician's individualized instructions.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: ACOG/SMFM advisory [1]; ACOG Committee Opinion 743 [2].</p>

<div class="eyebrow">PATIENT EDUCATION · SAFETY</div>

# Tell your clinician about allergies, bleeding, and medicines

<div class="grid three"><div class="card"><h2>Past reactions</h2><p>Report reactions to aspirin or anti-inflammatory medicines, including breathing problems.</p></div><div class="card"><h2>Bleeding or ulcers</h2><p>Tell your clinician about bleeding, stomach ulcers, or serious liver problems.</p></div><div class="card"><h2>Other medicines</h2><p>Bring a complete list, including blood thinners, over-the-counter products, and supplements.</p></div></div>
<p class="takeaway">Your clinician weighs the benefits and risks before recommending aspirin.</p>
<p class="source">Source: ACOG Committee Opinion 743, contraindications section [2].</p>

---
<div class="eyebrow">PATIENT EDUCATION · FOLLOW-THROUGH</div>

# Discuss anything that makes the daily plan difficult

<div class="grid three"><div class="card"><b class="number">01</b><h2>Access</h2><p>Can you obtain the medicine? Tell the team about cost or pharmacy barriers.</p></div><div class="card"><b class="number">02</b><h2>Routine</h2><p>Ask about a reminder that fits your day and what to do if you miss a dose.</p></div><div class="card"><b class="number">03</b><h2>Questions</h2><p>Share side effects or concerns. Do not change the dose on your own.</p></div></div>
<p class="takeaway">Try teach-back: “Here is how I will follow the plan.”</p>
<p class="source">Project counseling aim [8]. Practical support and teach-back are implementation suggestions.</p>

---
<div class="eyebrow">PATIENT EDUCATION · MONITORING</div>

# Aspirin does not replace prenatal or postpartum checks

<div class="grid two"><div class="card emphasis"><h2>Prevention</h2><p>Follow the aspirin plan if your clinician recommends it.</p></div><div class="card"><h2>Detection</h2><p>Keep appointments and blood pressure checks. Follow any home-monitoring instructions.</p></div></div>
<p class="takeaway">Before leaving, know your follow-up date and who to contact with concerns.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: ACOG patient FAQ [5]; USPSTF blood pressure screening recommendation [6].</p>

<div class="eyebrow">PATIENT EDUCATION · ACT RIGHT AWAY</div>

# Warning signs need medical care right away

<div class="grid two"><div class="card"><h2>Headache or vision changes</h2><p>A severe headache that persists or worsens; blurred vision or seeing spots.</p></div><div class="card"><h2>Severe pain or breathing trouble</h2><p>Severe belly pain, trouble breathing, or chest pain.</p></div></div>
<p class="takeaway">Contact your care team immediately. If you cannot reach them, go to the emergency department. Call 911 for a life-threatening emergency.</p>
<p class="small">These are examples, not a complete symptom list. Tell the team you are pregnant or recently gave birth.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: CDC Hear Her warning signs [7]; ACOG patient FAQ [5].</p>

<div class="eyebrow">PROVIDER TOOLKIT · HIGH RISK</div>

# One high-risk factor supports an aspirin recommendation

<div class="grid three compact"><div class="card"><h2>Prior preeclampsia</h2><p>Especially with an adverse outcome.</p></div><div class="card"><h2>Multifetal gestation</h2><p>Twins or higher-order multiples.</p></div><div class="card"><h2>Chronic hypertension</h2><p>Hypertension before pregnancy.</p></div><div class="card"><h2>Pregestational diabetes</h2><p>Type 1 or type 2.</p></div><div class="card"><h2>Kidney disease</h2></div><div class="card"><h2>Autoimmune disease</h2><p>Such as SLE or antiphospholipid syndrome.</p></div></div>
<p class="takeaway">Confirm the history and review contraindications before prescribing.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: ACOG/SMFM advisory [1]; USPSTF recommendation [3].</p>

---
<div class="eyebrow">PROVIDER TOOLKIT · MODERATE RISK</div>

# Assess moderate risk and social context

<div class="grid two"><div class="card"><h2>Multiple factors</h2><p>First pregnancy; prepregnancy BMI &gt;30; age ≥35; mother/sister with preeclampsia; IVF; personal history factors.</p><p>Personal history includes low birth weight/SGA, prior adverse pregnancy outcome, or &gt;10-year pregnancy interval.</p></div><div class="card emphasis"><h2>Equitable assessment</h2><p>Black racial identity and lower income are also moderate-risk factors. ACOG/SMFM allows consideration with either alone.</p><p>The risk reflects structural inequities and racism, not biological race.</p></div></div>
<p class="takeaway">Recommend aspirin with more than one moderate-risk factor; individualize and document the assessment.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: ACOG/SMFM updated advisory [1]; USPSTF [3].</p>

<div class="eyebrow">CLINICAL PEARLS · FIVE TAKEAWAYS</div>

# Identify risk early and close the loop

<ol class="pearls"><li><strong>Screen early:</strong> use a complete history and revisit uncertainties.</li><li><strong>Confirm eligibility:</strong> consider both risk factors and contraindications.</li><li><strong>Optimize timing:</strong> plan the start, dose, and duration explicitly.</li><li><strong>Support daily use:</strong> ask about access, concerns, and follow-through.</li><li><strong>Keep monitoring:</strong> explain urgent signs and the follow-up plan.</li></ol>
<p class="source">Sources: ACOG/SMFM [1]; ACOG [2]; USPSTF [3,6]; CDC [7].</p>

<div class="eyebrow">EVIDENCE + CONTROVERSIES · PROVIDERS</div>

# Aspirin is supported; higher doses and implementation need scrutiny

<div class="grid three"><div class="card"><h2>Established</h2><p>Randomized-trial synthesis supports preventive aspirin in increased-risk pregnancies. US guidance recommends 81 mg.</p></div><div class="card"><h2>Dose uncertainty</h2><p>A 2026 trial did not establish superior efficacy of 162 mg over 81 mg and reported an abruption imbalance.</p></div><div class="card"><h2>Next research</h2><p>Does the paper workflow improve screening, counseling, timely initiation, and equitable access?</p></div></div>
<p class="takeaway">Research on higher doses does not make 162 mg the default for this project.</p>
<p class="source">Sources: ACOG/SMFM [1]; Henderson et al. [4]; Khander et al., 2026 [9]. No completed Kendrick outcomes [8].</p>
