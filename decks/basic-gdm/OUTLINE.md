# Understanding Gestational Diabetes: Your Plan for Pregnancy and Beyond

Proposed rebuild under OpenMFM Presentation Standard v1.0. Prepared October 28, 2026. Status: approved by the user; implemented October 28, 2026. See QA.md for the completed review.

## Educational design

- Audience: patients and families, with clearly labeled expandable clinician notes for diagnostic criteria and guideline nuance.
- Central message: GDM can be managed through a practical daily plan, individualized pregnancy care, and follow-up after birth.
- Prerequisites: no medical knowledge assumed; define glucose, insulin, placenta, and glucose tolerance testing.
- Misconceptions to address: personal blame; abnormal screening equals diagnosis; all carbohydrates must be avoided; medication means failure; every patient needs identical testing or early delivery; diabetes risk ends at birth.
- Decisions: complete the correct diagnostic test, use the agreed glucose timing, review persistent elevations, choose medication with the care team, individualize delivery, and complete postpartum testing.
- Six-month takeaway: know your targets, ask for help when patterns change, and keep the postpartum glucose appointment.
- Presentation density: one main message, usually 30–60 visible words per slide; short headings, generous spacing, and optional details that also print. References and diagnostic tables may need more text.
- Visual direction: high-contrast OpenMFM dark palette, cyan/teal accents, simple HTML/CSS diagrams, meaningful headings, and no decorative medical stock imagery.

## Corrections and preservation plan

Preserve all valid original topics: mechanism, risk factors, screening, A1/A2 terminology, glucose goals, fingerstick/CGM discussion, nutrition, activity, medication, growth and antenatal surveillance, delivery, newborn care, feeding support, postpartum testing, and future diabetes prevention.

Correct or qualify these original claims:

- Remove unsupported prevalence/trend figures, “pancreas works 30% harder,” and “more than half have no risk factors” unless a matching reliable source is verified.
- Use nonjudgmental language without implying lifestyle has no influence or describing GDM as an inevitable normal condition.
- Explain that 24–28 weeks is the routine testing window, not the only time hyperglycemia develops.
- Replace fixed medication-use proportions with individual need and response.
- Distinguish SMFM's metformin position from ADA/ACOG's preference for insulin.
- Remove claims that fingersticks are required for every treatment decision or that sensor lag is invariably 15–20 minutes.
- Replace rigid food prohibitions and compulsory snack intervals with individualized nutrition advice.
- Remove universal growth scans at 32 and 36 weeks and universal surveillance schedules.
- Remove “placenta may age faster” and a blanket 37–38-week delivery rule for all complications.
- Label intrapartum glucose targets/check intervals as local protocols rather than universal patient instructions.
- Replace “50–70% within 10 years” and the fixed breastfeeding risk reduction with accurate, qualified long-term counseling; no unsupported numeric promises.
- Remove the unrelated SMFM Consult Series #58 citation and replace with the actual GDM pharmacotherapy statement.
- Avoid promising GDM always resolves; persistent postpartum diabetes must be recognized.
- Do not turn the pasted insulin/metformin starter doses into patient self-prescribing instructions. Explain treatment principles; prescribing belongs to the care team.
