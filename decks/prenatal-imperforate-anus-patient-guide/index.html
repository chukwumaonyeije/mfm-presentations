What do these words mean?

The anus is the opening where stool leaves the body

The rectum is the last part of the bowel. The anus is its opening to the outside.

In imperforate anus, that opening is missing or blocked.

This belongs to a group of conditions called anorectal malformations: differences in how the anus or rectum formed.

02 / 18Sources: 12

What is a fistula?

The bowel may connect through another small passage

A fistula is an abnormal passage from the bowel to the skin or a nearby urinary or genital structure.

Stool may pass through this small connection.

The exact connections matter for treatment.

How certain is the finding?

Ultrasound can raise concern without giving every answer

01

Clearly seen

The expected structures are visible. Some abnormalities can still be missed.

02

Limited view

Position or image quality prevents a clear look. This does not prove a problem.

03

Concerning appearance

A structure looks abnormal or is absent on a good view. More assessment is needed.

A scan usually cannot determine the exact anatomy or which operation may be needed.

What happens next?

A targeted MFM ultrasound is the next step

MFM means maternal-fetal medicine: specialists in pregnancies that need extra care.

01

Review

Look again at the original images and the reason for concern.

02

Focus

Take detailed views of the anal area and lower bowel.

03

Explain

Discuss what was seen, what was limited, and how confident the team is.

The team checks for a ring-like anal landmark, sometimes called the “target sign.” One sign alone does not give every answer.

Why examine more than the bowel?

Checking other organs helps guide care

The detailed scan also checks the heart, kidneys, bladder, spine, and limbs.

Other findings can change which tests are useful and where your baby should receive care.

Ask: “Is this the only finding, or are there other concerns?”

Will my baby need MRI or more scans?

Extra tests should answer a specific question

01

A view was limited

A repeat ultrasound may give a better look.

02

Pelvic anatomy is unclear

Fetal MRI may add detail in selected cases, especially when a complex connection is suspected.

03

Will it change the plan?

Ask how the result could affect counseling or newborn care.

MRI is not needed for everyone. Neither MRI nor ultrasound can rule out every abnormality.

Should I consider genetic testing?

Genetic counseling helps you understand your choices

A counselor can explain possible causes, testing options, and what results may mean.

01

Amniocentesis

A needle takes a small sample of fluid around the baby for diagnostic testing. Ask about benefits and procedure risks.

02

Chromosome microarray

A test on that sample looks for missing or extra pieces of chromosomes, which carry genetic information.

03

Your decision

Testing cannot find every condition. Some results are unclear. Screening blood tests provide different information.

Discuss what you hope to learn and whether testing would help you. Testing is a choice.

Does this change how or where I deliver?

Plan birth where your baby can get the right care

Arrange access to newborn specialists and pediatric surgery.

Birth at a specialist center may be advised. If another hospital is chosen, agree on a prompt transfer plan.

The finding alone does not automatically mean early birth or a cesarean. Your pregnancy needs and other findings guide that decision.

  1. Where? Hospital and specialist services
  2. When and how? A plan with your obstetric team
  3. Who? A named newborn care team
  4. What happens first? Examination and any transfer

What if my baby passes stool?

Your baby needs an examination soon after birth

An important detail

Passing stool does not rule out an abnormal connection.

01

Examine

Check the position and openness of the anus.

02

Assess

Look for an abnormal opening and other health concerns.

03

Act

Arrange imaging and prompt surgical assessment when needed.

Tell the newborn team about the prenatal concern, even if a later scan was more reassuring.

Will my baby need surgery right away?

Treatment depends on the anatomy found after birth

If imperforate anus is confirmed, surgery is needed. A blocked bowel may need urgent treatment; the full repair may happen later.

Newborn examination + imaging + the baby’s condition

Repair without a temporary colostomy

Possible for some babies, depending on the anatomy and their health.

Temporary colostomy, then repair

A belly opening drains stool into a bag. Repair and closure happen in later steps.

The surgeon chooses the plan after birth. The prenatal scan does not choose it.

What is life like after surgery?

Follow-up supports bowel function as your child grows

01

Healing

Your team teaches you the care needed after surgery.

02

Bowel care

Constipation or stool leakage may need medicines and an individual bowel-management plan.

03

Growing up

Follow-up adapts to toilet training, school, and your child’s needs.

Bowel control varies. The bowel anatomy, spine, and nerves affect the outlook. Ask for help early when problems arise.

What is known and what remains uncertain?

Some answers come before birth; others come later

Before birth

Build the care plan

Expert imaging can raise or lower concern, identify other findings, and help coordinate care.

After birth

Clarify the details

The examination and tests define the anatomy. The team chooses treatment and explains what may affect bowel function.

Research is improving prenatal detection. Studies are often small or include selected patients, so a scan cannot reliably predict every child’s outcome.

What should I remember?

Remember: review the scan, plan care, check after birth

01

Review the scan

Arrange targeted MFM assessment.

02

Plan care

Meet the team and make a birth plan.

03

Check after birth

Keep the newborn examination plan even if stool passes.

Why is the newborn examination still important?

A scan can miss details, and stool may pass through a fistula. The examination helps confirm the opening and guide further care.

If treatment is needed, keep follow-up visits as your child grows.
