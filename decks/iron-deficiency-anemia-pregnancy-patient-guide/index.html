OPENMFM / 02
Understand / WHAT IRON DOES

Iron helps your blood carry oxygen.

Your body uses iron to make hemoglobin (HEE-muh-glow-bin).

Red blood cells use hemoglobin to move oxygen.

Iron is the building block.

Without enough iron, your body may make too little hemoglobin.

Less oxygen may reach your tissues.

That can leave you tired or short of breath.

Sources: [1] O’Toole et al., 2024 [3] ACOG PB 233
OPENMFM / 03
Understand / WHY IT HAPPENS

Pregnancy makes your body need more iron.

Your body is doing extra work to support your baby.

♥

More blood

Your blood supply grows during pregnancy.

✧

Growing baby

Your baby also needs iron to grow.

●

The placenta

The placenta helps support your baby.

Low iron is a health problem—not a personal failure.
Sources: [1] O’Toole et al., 2024 [3] ACOG PB 233
OPENMFM / 04
Understand / HOW YOU MAY FEEL

Low iron can make you feel worn out.

Some people notice symptoms. Others feel normal.

☾

Very tired

Less energy than usual

◌

Dizzy

Lightheaded at times

〰

Out of breath

Especially with activity

♥

Fast heartbeat

Heart feels like it is racing

Symptoms can have many causes. Blood tests help find out why.
Sources: [1] O’Toole et al., 2024 [5] MedlinePlus, iron supplements [7] CDC HEAR HER
OPENMFM / 05
Understand / YOUR BLOOD TESTS

Two blood tests help find the cause.

Your care team may check these two numbers.

BLOOD COUNT

Hemoglobin

Shows how much oxygen-carrying protein is in your blood.

Think: How much can my blood carry?
IRON TEST

Ferritin

Shows how much iron your body has stored.

Think: How much iron is saved?
Not all anemia is caused by low iron. Your team checks the reason.
Sources: [1] O’Toole et al., 2024 [3] ACOG PB 233
OPENMFM / 06
Understand / WHY TREATMENT MATTERS

Treating low iron can protect your health.

Very low iron can make pregnancy and recovery harder.

FOR YOU

More strength for pregnancy and birth

Treatment builds your iron supply before you lose blood during delivery.

FOR YOUR BABY

Support for growth

Untreated anemia has been linked with early birth and low birth weight.

A higher risk does not mean a problem will happen. Treatment is a helpful step.
Sources: [1] O’Toole et al., 2024 [3] ACOG PB 233
OPENMFM / 07
Treat / YOUR OPTIONS

Iron pills are the usual first treatment.

Your best option depends on your tests, symptoms, and due date.

MOST OFTEN

Iron pills

First choice for many patients who can take them.

IF NEEDED
IV

Iron infusion

Iron goes into a vein when pills are not enough.

URGENT CASES
●

Blood transfusion

Used mainly for serious bleeding or severe illness.

These treatments do different jobs. Your team explains which one fits you.
Sources: [1] O’Toole et al., 2024 [2] FIGO, 2025 [4] WHO/FIGO/ICM, 2025
OPENMFM / 08
Treat / IRON TABLETS

Take the iron dose your care team chooses.

More pills do not always work better.

Know your schedule.

Your team may choose daily or every-other-day iron.

Prenatal vitamins may not be enough.

They usually contain less iron than is used to treat anemia.

Keep iron away from children.

Too much iron can be dangerous if swallowed by a child.

Good to know Do not start extra iron or change the dose without checking with your pregnancy care team.
Sources: [1] O’Toole et al., 2024 [2] FIGO, 2025 [5] MedlinePlus, iron supplements
OPENMFM / 09
Treat / TAKING YOUR IRON

A little planning helps your iron work.

Follow your prescription first. These tips may help.

✓ TRY THIS

Take it with water.

Iron often absorbs best on an empty stomach.

If it makes you sick, ask whether you can take it with a small snack.

⏱ SPACE IT OUT

Leave about 2 hours apart.

Milk, calcium pills, antacids, tea, and coffee can make iron harder to absorb.

Ask your pharmacist about other medicines.

Fruit with vitamin C can help you absorb iron from meals. Extra vitamin C pills are usually not needed.
Sources: [2] FIGO, 2025 [5] MedlinePlus, iron supplements [6] NIH Iron Fact Sheet
OPENMFM / 10
Treat / COMMON SIDE EFFECTS

Side effects have solutions.

Talk with your team if iron pills are hard to take.

◔

Upset stomach

A small snack may help.

≋

Constipation

Drink water and eat fiber.

●

Dark stools

This is often normal with iron.

Still having problems?Your team may change the type, timing, or schedule. Do not double a missed dose.
Good to know Black, sticky stools with weakness or dizziness, or any blood in the stool, need prompt medical assessment rather than being assumed to be an iron side effect.
Sources: [1] O’Toole et al., 2024 [5] MedlinePlus, iron supplements

Treat / FOOD AND IRON

Iron-rich foods can support your treatment.

Food matters, but food alone may not fix anemia quickly.

Choose iron-rich foods

BeansLentilsTofuFortified cerealLeafy greensFish or poultry
+

Add foods with vitamin C

Oranges, tomatoes, peppers, and broccoli can help your body use iron from food.

OPENMFM / 12
Treat / WHEN TABLETS ARE NOT ENOUGH

An iron infusion may be another option.

An infusion gives iron through a small tube in a vein.

Pills upset your stomach.

You cannot take enough iron by mouth.

Pills are not working.

Your blood test still shows anemia.

Your due date is near.

More treatment may be needed sooner.

Infusions are usually considered after the first three months of pregnancy.
Sources: [1] O’Toole et al., 2024 [2] FIGO, 2025 [4] WHO/FIGO/ICM, 2025
OPENMFM / 13
Treat / WHAT AN INFUSION IS LIKE

Your team will watch you during an infusion.

Iron enters your blood through an IV. You will be monitored.

01

Before

Your team reviews your blood tests, medicines, and past reactions.

02

During

A nurse gives iron through a vein. It may take one or more visits.

03

After

You are watched for reactions. The iron takes time to build new blood cells.

Tell the nurse right awayif you have trouble breathing, itching, chest tightness, or pain where the IV enters.
Sources: [1] O’Toole et al., 2024 [2] FIGO, 2025 [4] WHO/FIGO/ICM, 2025
OPENMFM / 14
Treat / IMPORTANT DIFFERENCE

A blood transfusion is not the same as iron.

They treat different problems.

IRON INFUSION

Builds iron stores

Gives your body iron so it can make red blood cells over time.

It does not work right away.
BLOOD TRANSFUSION

Gives red blood cells now

May be needed for major bleeding or severe symptoms.

It does not fully replace your missing iron.
Most stable patients with low iron do not need a blood transfusion.
Sources: [1] O’Toole et al., 2024 [2] FIGO, 2025
OPENMFM / 15
Follow through / CHECKING PROGRESS

Your blood test helps show if iron works.

Iron treatment takes time. Your team checks your progress.

START

Begin treatment

Take iron as directed or complete your infusion plan.

2–4
WEEKS

Check blood again

Your team may repeat the blood count to look for improvement.

LATER

Rebuild iron stores

You may need iron even after your blood count improves.

Ask exactly when your next blood test is due.
Sources: [1] O’Toole et al., 2024 [2] FIGO, 2025
OPENMFM / 16
Follow through / IF YOUR NUMBERS STAY LOW

If pills are not helping, tell your team.

There are several reasons iron treatment may not work.

TELL YOUR TEAM

What has been hard

Missed pills, nausea, constipation, trouble paying for medicine, or a hard schedule.

ASK WHAT COMES NEXT

What else needs checking

Other medicines, trouble absorbing iron, bleeding, or another cause of anemia.

The plan can change. You do not have to keep taking a pill that makes you sick without speaking up.
Sources: [1] O’Toole et al., 2024 [2] FIGO, 2025 [5] MedlinePlus, iron supplements
OPENMFM / 17
Follow through / WARNING SIGNS

Some symptoms need help right away.

These warning signs matter during pregnancy and after birth.

CALL 911 / EMERGENCY

Get emergency help for:

  • Chest pain
  • Severe trouble breathing
  • Fainting or collapse
  • Heavy bleeding with weakness or dizziness
CALL MATERNITY CARE NOW

Seek urgent care for:

  • Vaginal bleeding
  • Baby moving less than usual
  • Severe or lasting dizziness
  • New, extreme weakness
Do not wait for a routine visit. If you cannot reach your team, go for urgent care.
Sources: [7] CDC HEAR HER

Your iron plan should fit your pregnancy.

Your blood tests

◷

Your timing

Your daily life
