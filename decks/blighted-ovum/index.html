Why one scan may not be enough

One empty-looking sac can represent different moments

01

The pregnancy is earlier than expected

Ovulation or implantation may have happened later than calendar dating suggests.

Answer: repeat ultrasound
02

An empty-sac loss is confirmed

Strict ultrasound measurements or time-based criteria show that development has stopped.

Answer: discuss options
03

The location is not yet certain

If a definite pregnancy inside the uterus has not been seen, ectopic pregnancy remains possible.

Answer: follow location closely

Certainty before treatment

Repeat imaging is a safety step, not a delay without purpose

First scan

The team records what is visible and measures the sac or embryo.

→
WAIT
→
Repeat scan

Enough time passes for a real developmental change to become visible.

A diagnostic measurement

A mean sac diameter of at least 25 mm with no embryo meets the conservative SRU criterion for early pregnancy loss.

A time-based diagnosis

What was seen on the first scan determines the safe interval. Some systems also request a second opinion or repeat scan even at the size threshold.

Use the right test for the right question

Blood tests can guide follow-up, but they do not replace the scan

Serial hCG can show a trend

A trend can guide the next step when location or development is unclear. One number cannot prove where the pregnancy is or whether it will continue.

Confirm · Triage · Choose

Wait

All three accepted options work for most patients. They differ mainly in timing, predictability, and experience.

Option 1 · Expectant management

Waiting lets the body pass the pregnancy naturally

⌁The timeline is flexible
What it offersNo medication and no planned procedure.
What to expectThe timing is unpredictable. Cramping and bleeding may become heavy while tissue passes.
What makes it safeA contact number, clear warning signs, a follow-up plan, and a backup option.
You can change courseYou may choose medication or aspiration later if waiting becomes too difficult or does not work.
Option 2 · Medical management

Medication offers more control over timing

Step one

Mifepristone

When available, a clinician-prescribed dose prepares the uterus and improves the chance that treatment works promptly.

→
Step two

Misoprostol

The next medication causes cramping and bleeding so the pregnancy tissue can pass. Misoprostol alone is an accepted alternative.

Option 3 · Procedural management

Uterine aspiration is the fastest, most predictable option

01PrepareYour clinician explains pain control or anesthesia, the setting, and what to expect.
02AspirationGentle suction removes pregnancy tissue through the cervix.
03RecoverCramping and bleeding usually lessen after the procedure.
04Follow upYour team confirms recovery and reviews any testing that was clinically indicated.

May be chosen

For speed, predictability, or personal preference.

May be urgently needed

For severe bleeding, instability, infection, or another medical concern.

Your safety plan

Know which symptoms cannot wait

!Fainting, severe weakness, or trouble breathing
!Severe or worsening abdominal or one-sided pain
!Shoulder pain, especially with dizziness
!Very heavy bleeding or feeling unsafe at home
!Fever or chills
!Foul-smelling discharge or feeling dangerously unwell

Leave with exact instructions

Your team should give you a phone number, an after-hours plan, and a specific bleeding threshold for urgent evaluation.

If you feel faint or seriously ill, seek emergency care now.

Questions to ask your obstetric or MFM specialist

1Is this diagnosis certain, or do I need another ultrasound?
2Has the pregnancy been definitely located inside the uterus?
3What did the sac measure, and which diagnostic criterion was met?
4Am I medically stable enough to choose among waiting, medication, and aspiration?
5What should I expect for pain, bleeding, and timing with each option?
6Exactly when should I call you, and when should I go to emergency care?
7How will we confirm that treatment or natural passage is complete?
8Do Rh testing or Rh immune globulin recommendations apply to me?

You should leave with diagnostic certainty, a care plan, an emergency plan, and a clear method of follow-up.
