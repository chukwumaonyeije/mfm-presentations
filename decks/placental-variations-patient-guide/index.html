OPENMFM / 01

The basics

The placenta supports your baby’s growth.

The placenta supplies oxygen and nutrients through the umbilical cord.

Uterus: the womb where your baby grows.

Membranes: the thin sac around your baby, also called the bag of waters.

Cervix: the opening at the bottom of the uterus.

OPENMFM / 02

Two different findings

A folded edge and an extra lobe are different findings.

Circumvallate placenta

A folded edge

The membranes fold back at the placental edge, creating a raised rim.

Succenturiate lobe

An extra piece

A smaller piece of placenta is linked to the main placenta by blood vessels.

The name helps describe the shape. The scan details help guide care.

OPENMFM / 04

Care during pregnancy

A suspected folded edge often allows usual prenatal care.

Everything else is reassuring

→

Usual prenatal visits may be enough.

Growth becomes a concern

→

Your team can arrange a growth scan and adjust care.

Bleeding, pain, or fluid leakage

→

Get assessed promptly. Do not wait for the next scan.

Extra testing for the folded edge alone has no established benefit.
OPENMFM / 05

Succenturiate lobe

An extra lobe means checking where the vessels run.

The key question is how the connecting vessels relate to the cervix.

Color Doppler is an ultrasound method that shows blood flow.

A vaginal ultrasound gives a closer view of the cervix when checking for nearby vessels.

The team also checks both placental pieces and where the cord attaches.

OPENMFM / 06

Location matters

Vasa previa means the baby’s vessels lie near the cervix.

Vasa previa

The baby’s exposed vessels pass over or very near the opening. They may tear if the waters break.

Placenta previa

The placenta itself covers the opening. Either the main placenta or an extra lobe can be involved.

These are different diagnoses. Your team checks for both.
OPENMFM / 07

Planning birth

The scan findings help shape your birth plan.

Extra lobe, with no vasa previa or other reason for cesarean

→

Vaginal birth may be an option.
The lobe alone does not require surgery.

Confirmed vasa previa

→

Planned cesarean before labor or waters breaking.
Your specialist sets the timing.

Placenta still covering the cervix later in pregnancy

→

Cesarean birth is needed for persistent placenta previa.

If vessel location is unclear, further imaging comes before a final birth plan.
OPENMFM / 08

Your follow-up

Follow-up fits your whole pregnancy.

There is no one extra-testing schedule for every placental variation.

01

Location

Are the vessels and placental tissue clear of the cervix?

02

Growth

Is there a reason to check the baby’s growth again?

03

Symptoms

Have bleeding, leaking fluid, pain, or movement changes affected the plan?

If both variations are suspected, checking the extra lobe’s vessels remains essential.
OPENMFM / 09

When to get help

Bleeding or leaking fluid needs prompt attention.

Contact maternity care now

  • Vaginal bleeding or leaking fluid
  • Regular painful contractions
  • Baby moving less than usual once you know their pattern

If you cannot reach your team, seek urgent medical care.

With known vasa previa

Bleeding, labor symptoms, or waters breaking need immediate hospital assessment.

Do not wait for your next appointment.

Emergency help: for heavy bleeding, fainting, or severe pain that does not stop, call 911 or your local emergency number.

Vasa previa may have no symptoms. Ultrasound is still needed to check vessel location.

OPENMFM / 10

After birth

The team checks that every piece has delivered.

Make sure your birth team knows about the extra lobe.

A piece left in the uterus can cause bleeding. The team inspects the delivered placenta and assesses any concerns.

An extra lobe alone does not mean you need a procedure after birth.

After going home, heavy bleeding or fainting needs immediate medical attention.
OPENMFM / 11

What we know · what remains uncertain

Research guides care, but cannot predict every pregnancy.

A suspected folded edge

The evidence is limited.

Ultrasound studies and studies confirming the shape after birth do not always agree.

One recent study found similar outcomes with and without a suspected folded edge. That does not prove there is no risk.

Confirmed vasa previa

The care plan is clearer.

Specialist guidance supports planned birth before labor or the waters break.

The timing and need for hospital care before birth are tailored to the pregnancy.

Your symptoms, imaging, and baby’s growth put the research in context.

OPENMFM / 13

Take these to your appointment

Questions to ask your MFM specialist

MFM means maternal-fetal medicine: care for higher-risk pregnancies.

  1. Which finding is suspected, and which is confirmed?
  2. Are the vessels and placental tissue clear of the cervix?
  3. Do I need another scan? What will it check?
  4. Does this change how or when I give birth?
  5. Which symptoms need help, and who do I call?
  6. Will my birth team know about the extra lobe?
