Learning objectives

Leave with a repeatable dating workflow.

Your task at a new visit is to establish where the assigned date came from.

Evaluate the history

Separate a recalled last menstrual period (LMP) from a reliable menstrual dating history.

Find the evidence

Identify the earliest adequate ultrasound or assisted reproductive technology (ART) record.

Resolve discrepancies

Use the appropriate gestational-age window and request clinician review when sources conflict.

Make the decision traceable

Document the final estimated due date (EDD), its basis and the communication plan.

Why it matters

One dating error propagates through care.

Gestational age is an input to decisions across the entire pregnancy.

Verified EDD→Gestational age today

Interpretation

Screening windows and fetal growth percentiles depend on the assigned age.

Action

Surveillance, corticosteroid decisions and delivery timing depend on the best obstetric estimate.

A date copied forward without its source can carry uncertainty into every later visit.

Ask · Proposed interview workflow

A recorded LMP is not an evaluated LMP.

Ask about certainty and cycle behavior before using the date.

Ask these questions

What was the first day of your last normal period? How certain are you? What is your usual cycle length and range? Was that bleeding typical?

Record the context

Ask about irregular cycles, recent hormonal contraception, lactation or amenorrhea, and fertility treatment. Record unknown information as unknown.

Example: “LMP recalled with certainty; cycles vary from 35–60 days.” The recalled date is certain; the ovulation assumption is unreliable.

Ask · Reliability

Menstrual dating depends on assumptions.

LMP-based dating conventionally assumes a 28-day cycle and ovulation around day 14.

Regular, typical cycles

Day 1: LMP→~Day 14: ovulation

Even regular cycles do not prove the exact ovulation date.

Irregular or atypical cycles

Day 1?→Ovulation?

Variable cycles or atypical bleeding undermine the assumptions.

Do not use “28 ± 3 days” as a universal pass/fail rule. Document the actual pattern and reconcile with appropriate early imaging.

Retrieve · Proposed records workflow

Retrieve the earliest scan before accepting the referral EDD.

“Was there any ultrasound before the scan in this referral?”

  1. 01Early visit

    Emergency department, outside OB office or fertility clinic.

  2. 02Later scan

    Anatomy scan generates a new machine estimate.

  3. 03New referral

    The later estimate appears as the assigned EDD.

Request the original report: scan date, measurements, image adequacy, ultrasound age and the EDD adopted at that time. A patient’s recollection prompts retrieval; it does not substitute for a verified report.

Reconcile · Decision pathway

Reconcile the source before choosing the date.

An outside EDD is a starting point for review.

  1. 01ART record available

    Use the treatment-derived age; verify embryo age and transfer date.

  2. 02Reliable early dating available

    Check how it was established and retain it when later size differs.

  3. 03Dating not established

    Evaluate LMP reliability and the earliest adequate ultrasound together.

When records conflict or cannot be obtained, document the uncertainty and escalate to the responsible provider. Do not average competing EDDs.

Measure · First trimester

CRL quality determines the quality of early dating.

Crown–rump length (CRL) is only as useful as the image used to measure it.

Acquire before you calculate

Midsagittal view; neutral posture; sufficient magnification; longest straight crown-to-rump length. Exclude limbs and yolk sac.

Use technically adequate repeated measurements. Reacquire a flexed, extended or off-axis image.

Record image limitations. Do not assign confidence to an inadequate measurement simply because the machine displays a gestational age.

Atlanta Perinatal Associates · 8 / 25R3 ↗R6 ↗

Measure · Composite biometry

Composite biometry requires correct planes and calipers.

BPD + HC + AC + FL: preserve technique before interpreting the estimate.

BPD · Biparietal diameter

Thalami and cavum septi pellucidi visible; cerebellum excluded. Usually outer-to-inner skull edges; match the chart convention.

HC · Head circumference

Same axial plane; trace outer bony calvarium. Prefer HC when head shape distorts BPD.

AC · Abdominal circumference

True transverse section at stomach and portal sinus/umbilical vein level; trace the skin line.

FL · Femur length

Measure the ossified diaphysis; exclude the epiphysis. Avoid foreshortening.

Diagrams are measurement reminders, not anatomical reference images. Use the measurement convention validated for the selected chart.

*If the first ultrasound is in the third trimester, a difference >21 days may support redating. Use the whole clinical picture and follow-up because redating a small fetus can conceal growth restriction.

Reconcile · Protect the anchor

Redating criteria do not reset an established early EDD.

Use the table in the context of the original dating decision.

Initial dating assessment

Review LMP reliability, scan timing, technical quality and available prior studies. Reconcile the evidence into a best obstetric estimate.

Later growth assessment

Compare current size with the established age. A machine-generated “ultrasound age” is not automatically a replacement EDD.

Subsequent EDD changes should be rare, explained to the patient and clearly documented. A newly retrieved earlier report warrants review of the original decision, not an automatic overwrite.

Case 1 · Conflicting records

A later outside EDD may conceal an earlier scan.

Hypothetical referral: the chart lists an anatomy-scan EDD; the patient recalls an 8-week emergency visit.

  1. 01Retrieve

    Obtain the early report and confirm patient, scan date and measurement.

  2. 02Compare

    Was it an adequate CRL? What EDD was established? Why did the later report differ?

  3. 03Resolve

    Provider reconciles the record, documents the final basis and informs the referring team.

Until the discrepancy is resolved, clearly label the working date and outstanding evidence. Avoid silent changes in the ultrasound system.

Case 2 · Uncertain LMP

Uncertain LMP changes the interpretation.

Hypothetical case: 12w0d by uncertain LMP; technically adequate CRL corresponds to 10w3d.

Calculate

11 days
84 days − 73 days = 11 days.

Decide

Use the earliest reliable ultrasound estimate when LMP is uncertain. First check for an earlier scan or ART record.

Explain: “Your period date is uncertain. We are using the early ultrasound to establish the best estimate and recording its source.”

Case 3 · Arithmetic and boundaries

Check the arithmetic before checking the threshold.

Weeks-plus-days must be converted consistently.

9w0d versus 9w1d

1 day
63 versus 64 days. With reliable LMP and no other concern, this does not support redating.

At 10w0d by LMP

>7 days
Exactly seven days does not exceed the usual threshold. Eight days does.

The earliest window differs: before 9w0d, a discrepancy of more than five days supports redating. Clinical assessment of LMP reliability still matters.

Case 4 · Size versus age

A small fetus needs growth assessment, not a new birthday.

Hypothetical case: 32w0d by reliable early dating; current biometry corresponds to about 29 weeks.

Keep the dating anchor

Retain the established EDD. Confirm technique and assess estimated fetal weight (EFW), abdominal circumference (AC), interval growth and the clinical picture.

Evaluate the finding

SMFM defines FGR as EFW or AC below the 10th percentile for gestational age. “Measuring three weeks behind” alone does not establish that diagnosis.

Escalate suspected growth restriction for provider-directed assessment, including umbilical artery Doppler and surveillance when indicated. Redating can hide the abnormality.

Atlanta Perinatal Associates · 17 / 25R1 ↗

Special situation · Twins

Twins share one pregnancy date.

Measure each fetus and preserve the evidence for the pregnancy-level EDD.

Spontaneous conception

ISUOG recommends the larger CRL for dating in the first trimester. Record both measurements and establish chorionicity and amnionicity.

IVF conception

Use the ART-derived date. Do not create separate EDDs for each twin from their individual sizes.

Keep fetal labels consistent. Size discordance deserves assessment; assigning a younger date to the smaller twin can obscure the difference.

Special situation · Suboptimal dating

Missing early confirmation creates dating uncertainty.

Suboptimally dated: no ultrasound confirmed or revised the EDD before 22w0d.

Use the best clinical estimate

No elective delivery based on uncertain dating. Medically indicated care still follows the best clinical estimate and the clinical indication.

Plan follow-up

Consider interval ultrasound in 3–4 weeks. Consider surveillance at 39–40 weeks; delivery at 41 weeks is indicated using the best estimate.

Document uncertainty and individualize the plan. A repeat scan evaluates interval growth; it does not recover first-trimester dating precision. Routine amniocentesis for lung maturity is not recommended.

Suggested documentation template for a new-visit dating review.

This does not show whether menstrual history, outside studies or conflicting dates were reviewed.

Cycles: [usual length, range, regularity].

Communicate · Proposed handoff

Close the loop with the provider and patient.

Suggested division of work; follow local scope and reporting policy.

  1. 01Sonographer

    Collect history, document measurements and sources, flag missing or conflicting evidence.

  2. 02Provider

    Review the discrepancy, assign or confirm the EDD, record the clinical rationale and plan.

  3. 03Team + patient

    Update the shared record, inform the referring team and explain the basis of the final date.

Escalate poor image quality, unresolved source conflicts, ART discrepancies and concerning size findings. Avoid reassuring that “everything is normal” on dating information alone.

Quality improvement · Local proposal

Audit the process that produces the date.

Proposed local quality-improvement measures, not national performance mandates.

History completeness

Among new visits, how many document LMP certainty and cycle pattern, or why the history is unavailable?

Evidence retrieval

How often is the earliest report identified? Track unresolved requests and their follow-up.

Decision traceability

Can a reviewer reconstruct why the EDD was retained or changed?

Consistency

Do the ultrasound report, clinical chart and referring team use the same reconciled EDD?

Define a sample and denominator, measure a baseline, then choose local improvement targets. Audit late changes for rationale rather than assuming every change is wrong.

Clinical pearls: verify, document, preserve.

Six habits worth carrying into the next new visit.

  • Retrieve the earliest adequate scan.

  • Check the discrepancy in days.
  • Do not redate to normalize abnormal growth.

Evidence & controversies

Evidence and controversies: precision has limits.

Guidelines support consistency; they do not remove every source of uncertainty.

  1. 01Established

    Early ultrasound is more precise than late dating; reliable early dates should anchor later growth assessment.

  2. 02Context matters

    Cutoffs combine evidence and expert judgment. Uncertain LMP, late first scans and inadequate records require individualized interpretation.

  3. 03Improve the system

    Study whether structured histories and report retrieval improve documentation and reduce inappropriate EDD changes.

The five-step workflow and audit measures are educational proposals. Their effect on patient outcomes has not been established by this presentation. Twin dating recommendations also include lower-certainty evidence (ISUOG grade C for larger CRL).
