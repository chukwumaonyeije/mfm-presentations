# Fetal Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy: Thick Heart Muscle, Fluid Around the Heart, and What Happens Next

*Understanding a pre-hydropic fetal cardiac finding — for parents who have just been told.*

Status: outline presented to the user on September 11, 2026; user elected to proceed directly to production without a separate approval round. Implemented as `index.html` under OpenMFM Presentation Standard v1.0.
Prepared: September 11, 2026. Targeted evidence check: September 11, 2026; not a systematic literature search.
Audience: patients (expectant parents) who have received this diagnosis. A clinician companion deck was offered and not commissioned.

## Educational design

- Audience: parents told their baby has thickened ventricular walls and septum, a pericardial effusion, placentomegaly and borderline polyhydramnios — a pre-hydropic state. No prior medical knowledge assumed.
- Central message: a thick heart wall is a *finding*, not a final answer. The most important question is **why** it is thick, because that choice between "the heart is working harder than normal" and "the muscle is built differently" determines treatment and outlook.
- Duration: 23 slides, about 25–30 minutes read end to end; designed for repeat, selective reading after a consultation. Every slide fits a full-screen desktop viewport at 1568×904 except the five densest reference slides, which scroll slightly; content is fully reachable at every size tested.
- Prerequisites: none. Hypertrophy, cardiomyopathy, septum, pericardium, hydrops, Doppler, MoM, microarray and exome are each defined before use.
- Misconceptions addressed: that a thick heart in a fetus is the same disease as adult HCM; that "hydrops" has already happened; that the fluid around the heart must be drained; that a normal karyotype or microarray excludes a genetic cause; that this was caused by something the mother did; that the thickened muscle can be "fixed" before birth.
- Key decisions the family faces: consenting to the maternal laboratory panel, accepting or declining amniocentesis, accepting transfer of care to a tertiary/quaternary center, the monitoring interval, possible fetal therapy (transfusion, pericardiocentesis), and delivery timing, mode and site.
- Concepts that need diagrams rather than text: wall/septal thickening on a four-chamber view; the pericardial sac and its fluid layer; the body compartments that define hydrops; the venous-congestion pathway linking heart strain to placentomegaly and polyhydramnios; the MCA-PSV threshold; the 10-point cardiovascular profile score.
- Six-month takeaway: know that four findings are one story; know which causes are treatable and that they are tested for first; know the monitoring interval and the warning signs; know that delivery belongs at a center with pediatric cardiology, heart surgery and a level IV NICU.

## Provenance and editorial boundaries

Source material: the clinician's own presenting clinical reasoning supplied on September 11, 2026 — a 4.5 mm pericardial effusion with thickened ventricular walls and septum, early non-immune hydrops fetalis, placentomegaly (4.6 cm), borderline polyhydramnios (AFI 24.4 cm), and a four-step workup covering maternal laboratory testing, advanced fetal imaging with hemodynamic surveillance, invasive prenatal diagnostic testing, and clinical staging with management.

Editorial boundaries applied during production:

- The specific measurements are retained as a **teaching example** and are explicitly labelled as illustrative, so that no real patient record is reproduced and no identifiers appear.
- The supplied differential (22q13 deletion, trisomy 21, CMV, mitochondrial complex V deficiency) is presented at the level of chromosome differences, infection and metabolic/mitochondrial disease rather than as a ranked list of probabilities, because the source did not establish relative likelihoods.
- The chromosomal microarray → RASopathy panel → metabolic testing → exome sequencing sequence is presented as a tiered pathway, with the honest caveat that a normal result does not exclude a genetic cause, consistent with R4 and R16.
- No treatment is presented as reversing gene-driven myocardial hypertrophy before birth, because no such evidence exists at the time of writing; the deck states this limit explicitly.
- Outcome statements are framed by cause, not by a promise, and severe possibilities are named rather than softened.
