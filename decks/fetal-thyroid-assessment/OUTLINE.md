# Fetal Thyroid Assessment in Maternal Thyroid Disease

## OpenMFM Presentation Standard v1.0 — Outline for Approval

**Working title:** Fetal Thyroid Assessment in Maternal Thyroid Disease: Should We Evaluate the Neck?

**Subtitle:** A risk-based ultrasound strategy for physicians, advanced practice providers, and sonographers

**Audience:** Maternal-Fetal Medicine specialists, obstetric physicians, advanced practice providers, and MFM sonographers

**Format planned after approval:** Self-contained, responsive HTML presentation with keyboard/touch navigation, URL hash navigation, progress bar, slide counter, print/PDF mode, accessible diagrams, reduced-motion support, and a reference panel.

## Stage 0 — Educational Design

### Single most important message

Do not add serial fetal thyroid imaging for every maternal thyroid diagnosis. Evaluate the fetal thyroid and neck when maternal TRAb/TSI, antithyroid-drug exposure, uncontrolled hyperthyroidism, or the fetal phenotype creates a plausible risk of fetal thyroid dysfunction.

### What the learner should remember six months later

The maternal label is not the surveillance trigger. The fetal exposure is. Ask two questions: **Can stimulating antibodies reach this fetus? Can medication suppress this fetal thyroid?**

### Common misconceptions to correct

1. Maternal hypothyroidism after thyroidectomy or radioactive iodine means the fetus is also protected from hyperthyroidism.
2. Every patient taking levothyroxine needs a dedicated fetal neck study.
3. A fetal goiter tells us whether the fetus is hyperthyroid or hypothyroid.
4. Fetal tachycardia is the earliest or most sensitive sign of fetal hyperthyroidism.
5. Color Doppler alone can establish fetal thyroid function.
6. “Block-and-replace” is routine pregnancy therapy.
7. A large goiter automatically requires cesarean delivery or an EXIT procedure.

### Prerequisite knowledge

- Basic maternal thyroid physiology and the difference between Graves disease and Hashimoto thyroiditis
- Familiarity with TRAb/TSI, methimazole, carbimazole, PTU, and levothyroxine
- Standard fetal anatomy, growth assessment, heart-rate documentation, and color Doppler safety

### Key clinical decisions

1. Who needs fetal thyroid surveillance?
2. When should surveillance start and how often should it continue?
3. What constitutes an adequate targeted fetal thyroid examination?
4. Does the phenotype favor fetal hyperthyroidism, hypothyroidism, or remain indeterminate?
5. When should maternal therapy change?
6. When is invasive fetal testing justified?
7. Does the neck mass create a delivery-airway problem?

### Concepts that require diagrams

- Maternal antibody and medication pathways across the placenta
- Risk-triage decision tree
- Transverse fetal-neck anatomy and thyroid measurement plane
- Hyperthyroid versus hypothyroid phenotype matrix
- Multidisciplinary management and delivery workflow

## Evidence Audit of the Supplied Notes

| Supplied concept | Assessment | Revision for the presentation |
|---|---|---|
| Current or prior Graves disease matters even after ablation or thyroidectomy | Consistent | Retain. Test TRAb/TSI in the first trimester. The 2026 ATA threshold for fetal surveillance is sustained **>3× the assay upper limit of normal (ULN)**. |
| Elevated TRAb/TSI warrants serial fetal assessment | Consistent, but threshold and cadence need precision | Use monthly fetal ultrasound beginning at 18–20 weeks when TRAb/TSI remains >3× ULN. Repeat antibodies at 18–22 and 30–34 weeks when indicated. |
| Antithyroid medication exposure should prompt “consideration” of targeted imaging | Too weak under current guidance | The 2026 ATA guideline states that **all pregnant patients using antithyroid drugs require fetal thyroid ultrasound monitoring**, generally monthly from 18–20 weeks; frequency may be reduced for low-dose therapy case by case. |
| Stable Hashimoto hypothyroidism on levothyroxine does not require routine serial fetal thyroid imaging | Consistent | Retain. Standard anatomy evaluation remains appropriate unless another exposure, antibody risk, or fetal finding changes the risk assessment. |
| Fetal thyroid function begins at 18–20 weeks | Needs correction | Separate organ development from surveillance timing. The fetal thyroid develops and begins hormone synthesis earlier; **18–20 weeks is the recommended clinical start for targeted ultrasound surveillance in at-risk pregnancies**, not the onset of all fetal thyroid function. |
| Goiter, tachycardia, FGR, advanced bone age, cardiac dysfunction, hydrops, and fluid abnormalities are relevant | Consistent | Retain, but teach that tachycardia is a relatively late sign and goiter does not distinguish hyperthyroidism from hypothyroidism. |
| Central flow favors hyperthyroidism; peripheral flow favors hypothyroidism | Evidence-supported clue, not a stand-alone diagnosis | Present as one component of a composite phenotype with heart rate, bone maturation, movement, maternal therapy, and antibody status. |
| Maternal ATD can treat fetal hyperthyroidism | Consistent | Retain as multidisciplinary therapy. Add that ATD plus maternal levothyroxine is reserved for the unusual setting of fetal hyperthyroidism in a mother who is hypothyroid after definitive Graves treatment. Outside that setting, block-and-replace is not used in pregnancy. |
| Intra-amniotic levothyroxine may treat severe fetal hypothyroid goiter | Limited evidence | Label as selected fetal-therapy practice supported mainly by case series, not routine ACOG/SMFM guidance. First correct maternal ATD excess when applicable. |
| Large goiter may require tertiary delivery planning or EXIT | Consistent as risk-based expert practice | Retain, but avoid implying that size alone mandates cesarean or EXIT. Base planning on airway displacement/compression, neck extension, swallowing impairment, MRI/ultrasound findings, and local expertise. |

## Slide-by-Slide Outline

### Slide 1 — The maternal diagnosis does not answer the fetal question

- **Objective:** Frame the central clinical problem.
- **Key teaching point:** A mother may be hypothyroid while her fetus is hyperthyroid; fetal risk follows antibody and medication exposure, not the maternal diagnostic label alone.
- **Visual concept:** Cinematic split image: maternal thyroid status on one side, placental exposure pathway and fetal thyroid on the other.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026; ACOG PB 223.

### Slide 2 — A treated Graves history can remain biologically active

- **Objective:** Use a short clinical case to expose the key misconception.
- **Key teaching point:** Prior radioactive iodine or thyroidectomy removes maternal thyroid function but may not remove circulating TRAb/TSI.
- **Visual concept:** Case card: euthyroid on levothyroxine after radioactive iodine, elevated TRAb/TSI, normal initial anatomy scan. Prompt: “Does this fetus need a thyroid assessment?”
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026, Recommendations Table 16 and Table 5; ACOG PB 223.

### Slide 3 — Learning objectives: identify, image, interpret, escalate

- **Objective:** Define the learner outcomes.
- **Key teaching point:** By the end, learners should be able to select patients for surveillance, perform the targeted examination, distinguish likely phenotypes, and trigger appropriate multidisciplinary care.
- **Visual concept:** Four-part pathway with icons: triage → scan → classify → act.
- **Anticipated references:** OpenMFM educational design; no clinical citation required.

### Slide 4 — The answer is yes, selectively

- **Objective:** Give the practical answer early.
- **Key teaching point:** Dedicated fetal thyroid/neck surveillance is indicated for antithyroid-drug exposure, sustained TRAb/TSI >3× ULN, and concerning fetal findings. It is not routine for uncomplicated, well-controlled hypothyroidism on levothyroxine.
- **Visual concept:** Three-lane signal: monitor / investigate now / routine anatomy only.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026; ACOG PB 223; ATA 2017 for continuity with prior US practice.

### Slide 5 — Two placental pathways create opposite fetal phenotypes

- **Objective:** Explain mechanism without excessive endocrinology detail.
- **Key teaching point:** Stimulating antibodies can drive fetal hyperthyroidism; antithyroid drugs and excess iodine can suppress fetal hormone production and cause hypothyroid goiter.
- **Visual concept:** Placenta diagram with cyan “stimulate” pathway and amber “suppress” pathway.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026, fetal/neonatal considerations; ACOG PB 223.

### Slide 6 — Risk is defined by exposure, control, and phenotype

- **Objective:** Build the indication checklist.
- **Key teaching point:** Targeted fetal thyroid surveillance is appropriate with (1) ATD use, (2) TRAb/TSI >3× ULN, (3) uncontrolled maternal hyperthyroidism, or (4) fetal goiter, persistent tachycardia, growth restriction, cardiomegaly/heart failure, hydrops, abnormal bone maturation, or unexplained polyhydramnios.
- **Visual concept:** Converging three-input risk model: maternal history + laboratory/medication exposure + fetal findings.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026; ACOG PB 223; ACOG Committee Opinion 828 for individualized surveillance in poorly controlled thyroid disease.

### Slide 7 — Graves surveillance begins with antibodies, not ultrasound

- **Objective:** Define the laboratory-to-imaging timeline.
- **Key teaching point:** Measure TSH, FT4, and TRAb/TSI in the first trimester for every pregnant patient with current or prior Graves disease. If TRAb/TSI is >3× ULN, repeat at 18–22 and 30–34 weeks and perform fetal surveillance; if <3× ULN and the mother remains euthyroid, fetal hyperthyroidism monitoring can stop.
- **Visual concept:** Gestational timeline with decision gates at first trimester, 18–22 weeks, and 30–34 weeks.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026, Recommendations Table 16.

### Slide 8 — Antithyroid drugs require a fetal thyroid plan

- **Objective:** Clarify the updated strength of the indication.
- **Key teaching point:** When PTU, methimazole, or carbimazole continues in pregnancy, plan fetal thyroid ultrasound monitoring from 18–20 weeks, typically monthly; the cadence may be reduced for low-dose treatment after individualized review.
- **Visual concept:** Medication dose gauge linked to maternal FT4 target and fetal thyroid surveillance calendar.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026, Table 4 and Flowchart 4; ACOG PB 223.

### Slide 9 — Well-controlled Hashimoto disease does not justify serial neck imaging

- **Objective:** Prevent over-imaging.
- **Key teaching point:** Stable hypothyroidism treated with levothyroxine, including typical Hashimoto thyroiditis, does not independently require serial fetal thyroid ultrasound. Re-enter the high-risk pathway only if Graves history/TRAb, ATD or excess iodine exposure, maternal instability, or a fetal finding is present.
- **Visual concept:** “Routine pathway” card with explicit off-ramps back to targeted assessment.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2011 Recommendation 19; ATA 2026 sections on hypothyroidism and thyroid autoimmunity; ACOG PB 223.

### Slide 10 — Find the gland in the true transverse neck plane

- **Objective:** Teach reproducible image acquisition.
- **Key teaching point:** Obtain a transverse neck image below the mandible and above the clavicles, identify the trachea centrally and paired carotid vessels laterally, then document the bilobed thyroid anterior and lateral to the trachea.
- **Visual concept:** CSS/SVG anatomic diagram paired with a labeled image-placeholder frame for future sonographic teaching images.
- **Anticipated references:** Ranzini et al. 2001; AIUM detailed second/third-trimester practice parameter.

### Slide 11 — Measure size against gestational age, not visual impression

- **Objective:** Standardize thyroid biometry.
- **Key teaching point:** Record transverse dimensions and/or circumference/area using a validated gestational-age or BPD-based nomogram. Thyromegaly above the 90th–95th percentile supports goiter; the same threshold does not define function.
- **Visual concept:** Measurement overlay and compact percentile curve with “>95th” highlighted.
- **Anticipated references:** Ranzini et al. 2001; ATA 2026 Table 6.

### Slide 12 — A targeted thyroid scan is a fetal systems examination

- **Objective:** Expand the sonographer checklist beyond the neck.
- **Key teaching point:** Document thyroid size/shape, echogenicity, vascular pattern, tracheal relationship, neck position, swallowing, amniotic fluid, fetal heart rate, cardiac size/function, hydrops, growth, movement, and bone maturation.
- **Visual concept:** Radial checklist centered on the fetal thyroid.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026 Table 6; Huel et al. 2009; ATA 2017.

### Slide 13 — Goiter proves enlargement, not thyroid direction

- **Objective:** Prevent the most consequential interpretation error.
- **Key teaching point:** Both fetal hyperthyroidism and hypothyroidism can produce a goiter. Interpret enlargement in the context of maternal TRAb/TSI, ATD dose, fetal heart rate, vascularity, maturation, movement, growth, and cardiac status.
- **Visual concept:** One goiter splitting into two possible diagnostic paths.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026 Table 6; Huel et al. 2009.

### Slide 14 — The phenotype, not one Doppler pattern, guides the diagnosis

- **Objective:** Compare the likely hyperthyroid and hypothyroid patterns.
- **Key teaching point:** Hyperthyroidism is favored by central/diffuse flow, growth restriction, accelerated distal femoral ossification, hyperactivity, cardiomegaly/heart failure, hydrops, and persistent tachycardia. Hypothyroidism is favored by peripheral flow, delayed ossification, reduced movement, neck hyperextension/compression, and polyhydramnios. Overlap is common.
- **Visual concept:** Side-by-side phenotype matrix with a central “indeterminate” column.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026 Table 6; Huel et al. 2009.

### Slide 15 — Doppler is a clue with technical traps

- **Objective:** Teach safe, cautious Doppler interpretation.
- **Key teaching point:** Low velocity settings can reveal vascular distribution, but fetal movement and blooming can create false central flow. Use ALARA, minimize dwell time, and never classify fetal thyroid function from color Doppler alone.
- **Visual concept:** “True signal vs artifact” comparison with controls called out.
- **Anticipated references:** Huel et al. 2009; AIUM detailed ultrasound practice parameter.

### Slide 16 — Not every anterior neck mass is a thyroid

- **Objective:** Provide a focused differential diagnosis.
- **Key teaching point:** Distinguish thyroid enlargement from lymphatic malformation, cervical teratoma, foregut duplication cyst, thyroglossal duct cyst, hemangioma, and other neck masses by location, composition, vascularity, relationship to airway/esophagus, and associated findings.
- **Visual concept:** Location-and-composition differential map.
- **Anticipated references:** AIUM detailed ultrasound parameter; fetal neck-mass imaging reviews.

### Slide 17 — The report should answer the next clinical question

- **Objective:** Standardize communication.
- **Key teaching point:** A useful report states indication/exposure, thyroid biometry and percentile source, symmetry/echogenicity, vascular pattern, airway relationship, neck position, swallowing and fluid, heart rate, cardiac/hydrops findings, growth, bone maturation, impression, and escalation recommendation.
- **Visual concept:** Interactive-style report template with required fields.
- **Anticipated references:** AIUM documentation principles; ATA 2026.

### Slide 18 — Suspected fetal hyperthyroidism is treated through the mother

- **Objective:** Connect phenotype to management.
- **Key teaching point:** Maternal antithyroid therapy can treat confirmed or strongly suspected fetal hyperthyroidism. Dose changes require coordinated MFM-endocrinology management with maternal laboratory monitoring and serial fetal ultrasound.
- **Visual concept:** Maternal treatment → placenta → fetal response loop.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026, Recommendation Table 19; ACOG PB 223.

### Slide 19 — Block-and-replace has one narrow fetal-treatment exception

- **Objective:** Correct an important therapy misconception.
- **Key teaching point:** A hypothyroid mother after radioactive iodine or thyroidectomy may continue levothyroxine while receiving an ATD to treat fetal hyperthyroidism from persistent TRAb/TSI. Outside this scenario, block-and-replace therapy is not used in pregnancy.
- **Visual concept:** “Exception, not routine” decision card.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026, Recommendation Table 19.

### Slide 20 — Suspected fetal hypothyroidism starts with removing suppression

- **Objective:** Present a graded approach to fetal hypothyroid goiter.
- **Key teaching point:** Reassess and reduce maternal ATD or excess iodine exposure when clinically safe. Persistent severe goiter with compressive effects may prompt fetal-therapy consultation; intra-amniotic levothyroxine remains individualized and supported mainly by observational data and case series.
- **Visual concept:** Step-up ladder: correct exposure → reassess → multidisciplinary fetal therapy.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026; Huel et al. 2009; fetal goiter management review.

### Slide 21 — Invasive testing is reserved for consequential uncertainty

- **Objective:** Define when cordocentesis or amniotic testing may be considered.
- **Key teaching point:** If a goiter is present and fetal thyroid status remains unclear despite maternal history, antibodies, medication review, and ultrasound phenotype, invasive testing at 20–24 weeks may be considered only when the result will change treatment. Cordocentesis is more direct but carries material fetal risk.
- **Visual concept:** Benefit-risk balance with “Will the result change management?” as the gate.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026 fetal/neonatal considerations.

### Slide 22 — Delivery planning follows airway physiology, not goiter size alone

- **Objective:** Translate prenatal findings into peripartum planning.
- **Key teaching point:** Evaluate tracheal displacement/compression, neck extension, swallowing, polyhydramnios, and cardiac status. Large or obstructive lesions warrant tertiary delivery planning with neonatology, pediatric airway specialists, anesthesia, and fetal imaging; MRI and EXIT are selected tools, not automatic steps.
- **Visual concept:** Prenatal airway risk pathway ending in standard delivery, prepared neonatal airway support, or selected EXIT planning.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026; fetal goiter peripartum management review; fetal airway imaging review.

### Slide 23 — A single clinic algorithm aligns the whole team

- **Objective:** Integrate maternal triage, sonography, interpretation, and action.
- **Key teaching point:** History of Graves → first-trimester TRAb/TSI; ATD use or sustained >3× ULN → monthly ultrasound from 18–20 weeks; concerning fetal phenotype → classify, coordinate, treat, and plan delivery; stable Hashimoto/LT4 without risk modifiers → routine anatomy pathway.
- **Visual concept:** Full-width decision tree designed for future printing as a one-page clinic aid.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026; ACOG PB 223.

### Slide 24 — The opening case changes because the exposure was recognized

- **Objective:** Close the educational loop.
- **Key teaching point:** Maternal hypothyroidism after definitive Graves treatment did not remove fetal risk. Antibody testing selected the fetus for surveillance before a late fetal phenotype appeared.
- **Visual concept:** Before-and-after case timeline with decision points.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026.

### Slide 25 — Clinical pearls

- **Objective:** Reinforce memorable practice points.
- **Key teaching point:** Eight concise takeaways: risk follows exposure; prior Graves still matters; >3× ULN is the surveillance threshold; ATD use requires a fetal plan; start at 18–20 weeks; goiter is not a functional diagnosis; tachycardia is relatively late; airway planning is multidisciplinary.
- **Visual concept:** Eight high-contrast pearl cards, progressive reveal in screen mode and static grid in print mode.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026; ACOG PB 223.

### Slide 26 — Evidence and controversies

- **Objective:** Separate guideline-backed practice from lower-certainty areas.
- **Key teaching point:** Strongest consensus exists for antibody/ATD-based surveillance. Uncertainty remains around optimal thyroid nomograms, the diagnostic performance of Doppler phenotype scores, exact scan frequency for low-dose ATD, invasive-test selection, intra-amniotic levothyroxine regimens, and objective EXIT thresholds.
- **Visual concept:** Three-column evidence map: known / uncertain / research priorities.
- **Anticipated references:** ATA 2026; Huel et al. 2009; Ranzini et al. 2001; fetal goiter review.

### Slide 27 — References

- **Objective:** Provide complete, updateable citations and direct source links.
- **Key teaching point:** The deck is anchored to the 2026 ATA guideline, ACOG PB 223, AIUM imaging standards, and primary sonography literature. No separate current SMFM Consult Series devoted specifically to fetal thyroid surveillance was identified; the presentation will state this transparently instead of implying a stand-alone SMFM recommendation.
- **Visual concept:** Categorized references: guidelines / imaging standards / primary studies / fetal therapy and airway.
- **Anticipated references:** Complete list below.
