Clinical Decision Support
Rapid evidence retrieval and synthesis

Three tools built for the clinical question itself

EvidenceMD
evidencemd.com
Point of care

AI-powered clinical intelligence platform designed for physicians. Synthesizes current medical literature into evidence-based answers.

  • Rapid literature review
  • Guideline summaries
  • Complex case support with cited sources
OpenEvidence
openevidence.com
Peer reviewed

AI medical search engine trained on peer-reviewed literature and clinical guidelines. Strong for rapid sourced retrieval during consultations or rounds.

  • Referenced responses
  • Useful during rounds and consults
  • Free for verified physicians
UpToDate
uptodate.com
Gold standard

Comprehensive peer-reviewed point-of-care reference with integrated recommendations and drug references across specialties.

  • Topic summaries across specialties
  • Guideline-concordant practice support
  • Especially useful when breadth matters
When the question is “What does the evidence say right now?”, start here.
03
Specialty Anchors
OB/GYN and MFM guideline references

For specialty management decisions, keep ACOG and SMFM in front

ACOG Practice Bulletins & Committee Opinions
acog.org
Official guidance

Primary reference for OB/GYN and MFM management recommendations. Free summaries are available. Full text requires membership.

SMFM Consult Series
smfm.org
High risk obstetrics

Evidence-based consensus statements for high-risk obstetric conditions. These remain among our specialty's most authoritative guidance documents.

Question type Best anchor in this list
Broad clinical management or guideline-concordant practice UpToDate plus the relevant ACOG document
OB/GYN management recommendation or policy-style guidance ACOG Practice Bulletin or Committee Opinion
High-risk obstetric consensus statement for MFM care SMFM Consult Series
Bottom line: use AI search to get oriented, but anchor management decisions in specialty guidance when the stakes are high.
The specialty references in this deck are not optional background material.
04
MFM-Specific Tools
Patient education and surveillance support

Two resources with direct relevance to MFM workflow

OpenMFM.org
openmfm.org
Our platform

Open-source Maternal-Fetal Medicine patient education and clinical decision support platform developed by our practice, built for both patients and clinicians.

  • FGR management
  • Cervical length screening
  • Periviability counseling
  • cfDNA navigator and related microsites
PeriGen / Perinetalex
AI surveillance tools
Fetal monitoring

AI-assisted fetal monitoring and antepartum surveillance tools that may support NST interpretation and early detection of concerning patterns.

  • Useful in fetal surveillance settings
  • Supports pattern recognition
  • Should complement, not replace, bedside interpretation
Practice reminder: OpenMFM.org is our own platform. Share it with patients as appropriate, and flag any content that needs updating.
These tools are closest to patient education and specialty-specific bedside use.
05
General AI Assistants
Strong utility, but with clear boundaries

Helpful for drafting, summarizing, and research. Not clinical decision support per se.

Claude
claude.ai
Anthropic

Strong reasoning and structured writing assistant for consultation notes, patient letters, literature summaries, and educational content.

ChatGPT
chat.openai.com
OpenAI

Widely used assistant for drafting, brainstorming, and general information retrieval. Useful, but clinical outputs still need verification.

Perplexity AI
perplexity.ai
Cited web search

AI-powered search with real-time web retrieval and citations. Helpful for rapid background research with source attribution.

Guardrail: always review and verify AI-generated content before placing anything in the medical record.
These platforms are best used as supervised cognitive tools, not autonomous clinical authorities.
06
Education and Presentation
For teaching, visuals, and cleaner communication

Three practical resources for teaching and slide building

MedPix
medpix.nlm.nih.gov
NLM image library

Free radiologic and clinical image database from the National Library of Medicine, especially useful for teaching cases and presentation images.

Canva for Medicine
canva.com
Design platform

Useful for patient handouts, educational slides, and practice materials. The free tier is sufficient for most use cases.

SlideMD / Gamma
gamma.app
AI deck builder

Can generate structured slide decks from outlines or prompts. Useful for CME talks, grand rounds, and patient education presentations.

Teaching strategy: use MedPix for visuals, Canva for cleaner layouts, and AI deck builders when you need a fast first pass from an outline.
Good clinical teaching is not just accurate. It also needs visual clarity.
07
Practical Workflow
One way to use the list without mixing categories

A clean four-step workflow for responsible clinical use

Step 01

Ask the clinical question

Decide whether you need a guideline answer, a literature synthesis, a drafted explanation, or a teaching asset.

Step 02

Verify with evidence tools

Use EvidenceMD, OpenEvidence, UpToDate, ACOG, or SMFM to ground the answer in sources and specialty guidance.

Step 03

Adapt for communication

Use Claude, ChatGPT, or Perplexity to help draft summaries, patient letters, or internal teaching copy with oversight.

Step 04

Share with the right format

Use OpenMFM, MedPix, Canva, or SlideMD / Gamma to support patient education and cleaner educational delivery.

Important: this workflow is a practical presentation framework built from the listed tools. It is not a formal clinical guideline.
Keep the evidence layer separate from the communication layer.

Closing Reminders
What should stay with the audience

Three takeaways for safe and useful adoption

Supplement, do not replace

AI platforms should supplement clinical judgment, not replace it.

Review before documentation

Always review and verify AI-generated content before including it in the medical record.

Share OpenMFM and improve it

Use our platform with patients as appropriate, and keep feeding back gaps or updates that matter in practice.
