# Prenatal diagnosis of tetralogy of Fallot

Subtitle: A practical guide for maternal-fetal medicine clinicians and sonographers.

Status: approved for production September 17, 2026. User additionally requested an interactive clinician quiz with explanations and final score. Proposed new URL: /decks/tetralogy-of-fallot-prenatal-diagnosis/index.html.

## Educational design

- Central message: recognize the anatomic pattern, then assess pulmonary blood flow to plan care at birth.
- Audience: MFM clinicians, sonographers, and trainees familiar with basic fetal cardiac views.
- Six-month recall: a normal four-chamber view does not exclude TOF; diagnose across multiple views; document the ductus and pulmonary outflow; communicate actionable risk.
- Misconceptions: RV hypertrophy is necessary prenatally; a single vessel ratio or velocity proves TOF; a normal ductal-flow pattern excludes later ductal dependence; all cases need the same delivery or surgery plan.
- Key decisions: referral, subtype and differential diagnosis, genetic evaluation, reassessment, and neonatal care capability.
- Visual priorities: multiplanar anatomy, scan acquisition sequence, flow direction, phenotype comparison, and a delivery-risk pathway.
- Evidence labels: guideline recommendation, observational evidence, and practical scanning advice. Case exercises will be explicitly hypothetical.

## Required source corrections

1. Replace “diagnosis obligates amniocentesis” with an offer of diagnostic testing and informed counseling. Avoid implying that a normal CMA excludes all genetic disease.
2. Remove unsupported universal MPA:Ao and RVOT velocity thresholds. Show any research threshold with its cohort, gestational age, measurement method, outcome, and limitations.
3. Do not make TOF/DORV classification depend only on an override percentage; document ventriculoarterial connections and obtain expert adjudication.
4. Replace certainty about ductal dependence with integrated risk assessment. Normal fetal ductal flow does not guarantee a benign postnatal course.
5. Replace automatic PGE1 at cord clamping for every flagged case with a coordinated neonatal plan for suspected ductal-dependent pulmonary blood flow. Pulmonary atresia with collateral supply requires anatomical clarification.
6. Replace mandatory fixed follow-up and universal delivery-site rules with individualized cardiac and obstetric planning. A maternal facility level does not guarantee pediatric cardiac surgical capability.
7. Remove universal genetic prevalence and >98% survival claims unless a verified, population-matched source is provided. Distinguish surgical survival from long-term outcome.
8. Correct the old “stenosis protects fetal lungs” explanation: fetal stability principally reflects fetal circulation and placental gas exchange. Do not infer an individual fetus has no valve leak, no heart-failure risk, or non-ductal-dependent circulation.
9. Preserve correct AVSD anatomy and its chromosome association only as associated/differential findings. Do not retain a patient-specific suspected trisomy as a generic TOF diagnosis.
10. Replace generic or mislabeled references and unsupported reaffirmation dates with exact citations. Remove blanket HIPAA guarantees; state only the actual privacy scope of the educational artifact.
