MFM billing: answer key

2026 service-year edition · 16 questions · Reviewed November 23, 2026

Physician track: C1–C8 + P1–P4. APP track: C1–C8 + A1–A4. Fictional cases; no CME credit.

C1 · shared · RVUs & attribution

An MFM contract explicitly credits work RVUs. Which measure belongs in that productivity ledger?

  1. Total RVUs including practice expense

    Total RVUs include components outside this stated contract.

  2. Work RVUs credited under the contract

    Correct: the contract specifies work RVUs and its crediting rules.

  3. The amount the payer collected from the patient

    Patient payments are collections, not work RVUs.

  4. The Medicare conversion factor

    The conversion factor converts adjusted RVUs to a fee-schedule amount.

C2 · shared · RVUs & attribution

A fictional contract pays $55 per credited wRVU with no threshold. A visit receives 2 wRVUs. What compensation follows from these assumptions?

  1. $66.80

    This substitutes the rounded Medicare CF for the contract rate.

  2. $133.60

    This uses the slide’s separate payment example.

  3. $110.00

    Correct: 2 × $55 = $110. The assumptions exclude thresholds or other adjustments.

  4. $55.00

    This credits one wRVU rather than two.

C3 · shared · E/M & payer rules

A Medicaid patient is referred for an MFM opinion about maternal disease. What is the next coding step?

  1. Automatically use an outpatient consultation code

    Referral alone does not establish payer recognition.

  2. Use the highest new-patient code

    Neither patient status nor level is established by the referral.

  3. Apply Medicare consultation policy to every Medicaid plan

    Medicare policy is not a universal Medicaid rule.

  4. Check this payer’s code recognition and documentation requirements

    Correct: identify the plan’s policy before selecting the E/M family.

C4 · shared · E/M & payer rules

A stable patient’s chart carries a high-risk pregnancy diagnosis. What best supports office E/M level selection?

  1. Documented MDM or qualifying clinician time

    Correct: evaluate the work supported by today’s record.

  2. The high-risk diagnosis alone

    A risk label does not establish a complete E/M level.

  3. The length of the copied problem list

    Copied text is not evidence of work addressed today.

  4. The number of ultrasound images

    Image count is not an office E/M leveling criterion.

C5 · shared · E/M & payer rules

An MFM clinician spends 35 qualifying minutes on E/M and 10 additional minutes on separately billed scan interpretation. How much time enters the E/M calculation?

  1. 45 minutes

    This double counts separately reported work.

  2. 10 minutes

    This counts only the excluded service.

  3. 35 minutes

    Correct: count the 35 eligible E/M minutes.

  4. All time the patient spends in the clinic

    Waiting and staff time do not become clinician E/M time.

C6 · shared · Imaging & co-management

After a fetal anatomy scan, the physician only provides the explanation ordinarily included with the imaging service. What is the best billing conclusion?

  1. Every scan also supports a level-3 visit

    No independent E/M work has been established.

  2. A separate E/M service is not established by this description

    Correct: routine scan-related work alone does not show separate E/M.

  3. Modifier 25 creates a separate visit

    A modifier describes work; it does not create it.

  4. A second diagnosis guarantees E/M payment

    Diagnosis count cannot substitute for a separate service.

C7 · shared · Imaging & co-management

Another practice provides routine prenatal care while MFM manages a complication. How should the team assess billing?

  1. All MFM services are included in the other practice’s global fee

    This may conceal eligible separate work.

  2. All MFM services are separately payable

    This may improperly unbundle care.

  3. Use a global delivery code for every MFM visit

    The MFM visit does not establish delivery/global services.

  4. Clarify care responsibilities and payer/global-package rules

    Correct: determine which services were supplied and how the payer treats them.

C8 · shared · RVUs & attribution

An MFM claim was paid correctly, but the clinician’s production report lacks credit. What should happen first?

  1. Reconcile rendering identity and contract credit rules

    Correct: investigate the internal attribution mismatch.

  2. Submit the paid claim again

    Duplicate submission does not fix the productivity ledger.

  3. Add modifier 59 to generate credit

    Modifier 59 does not assign employment credit.

  4. Assume the payer owes more

    A crediting issue does not establish payer underpayment.

P1 · physician · Imaging & co-management

The hospital furnished ultrasound equipment and acquisition. The MFM physician furnished only interpretation and report. Which approach is appropriate?

  1. Bill global imaging because the report is signed

    Signing does not establish entitlement to both components.

  2. Bill only the technical component

    The physician furnished professional interpretation, not acquisition.

  3. Assess professional-component billing under the arrangement

    Correct: verify component eligibility and the billing arrangement.

  4. Add modifier 25 to the imaging code

    Modifier 25 describes qualifying E/M, not imaging ownership.

P2 · physician · Imaging & co-management

FGR ultrasound is followed by a separately documented management visit. What is the strongest approach to E/M billing?

  1. Add modifier 25 without reviewing the note

    The record must support separate work.

  2. Support the separate service and verify payer modifier requirements

    Correct: the service and policy determine the claim.

  3. Include scan interpretation time in E/M

    Separately reported scan time must be excluded.

  4. Use a different diagnosis solely to obtain payment

    Diagnosis manipulation does not establish eligibility.

P3 · physician · Team billing

A physician co-signs an APP’s Medicare office note but performs no additional work. Incident-to requirements have not been established. What is correct?

  1. The signature alone supports physician billing

    A signature alone does not establish the required service or supervision conditions.

  2. This is automatically a split/shared office visit

    Medicare office visits are not split/shared visits.

  3. The highest clinician credential determines billing

    Credential hierarchy does not replace billing requirements.

  4. The team must establish a valid pathway from the actual work

    Correct: assess direct APP billing or another pathway only if its conditions are met.

P4 · physician · Imaging & co-management

A fetal imaging claim has a procedure-to-procedure edit. What is the best next action?

  1. Check the current pair, modifier indicator and documented circumstances

    Correct: determine whether a supported modifier exception is available.

  2. Append modifier 59 to every denied imaging line

    A denial is not evidence of distinct services.

  3. Assume separate diagnoses override the edit

    Separate diagnoses alone do not satisfy modifier requirements.

  4. Replace the performed service with a higher-valued code

    Code the work performed, not a payment target.

A1 · app · Team billing

An enrolled MFM NP personally performs a medically necessary office visit within scope. Incident-to criteria are not met. What pathway may be available?

  1. No professional service can be billed

    Failure of incident-to eligibility does not eliminate direct billing eligibility.

  2. Only billing through the physician’s NPI

    A physician NPI is not universally required.

  3. Direct NP billing if the service meets payer requirements

    Correct: eligible personally furnished NP services can be directly billed.

  4. Automatic global maternity billing

    This encounter does not establish a global maternity service.

A2 · app · Team billing

An NP evaluates a new concern during established diabetes follow-up. The physician has not addressed that concern. What should the NP do about billing?

  1. Reassess incident-to eligibility and document the actual work

    Correct: evaluate the new problem clinically and verify the appropriate billing pathway.

  2. Use incident-to because the patient is established

    Patient status alone is insufficient.

  3. Obtain only a co-signature and assume eligibility

    A co-signature does not establish the required treatment involvement.

  4. Ignore the new concern to preserve the original billing plan

    Patient needs determine care; the billing plan must follow it.

A3 · app · Team billing

Which statement best describes Medicare’s typical direct professional-service allowed amounts for NPs/PAs and CNMs?

  1. Every APP receives 85% under every payer

    Both profession and payer matter.

  2. NP/PA generally 85%; eligible CNM services 100% of the physician fee-schedule basis

    Correct: these are Medicare allowed-amount rules, not universal payer or salary rules.

  3. All receive 100% if a physician is in the building

    Availability alone does not change the direct-billing percentage.

  4. The percentage equals the clinician’s take-home pay

    Employment compensation and allowed amounts are different measures.

A4 · app · Team billing

An APP and physician from the same group share an eligible hospital outpatient E/M encounter. What determines the Medicare split/shared billing clinician?

  1. Who opened the chart first

    Chart-opening order is not the criterion.

  2. Who has the higher salary

    Compensation does not determine the renderer.

  3. Who co-signed last

    Signature sequence does not establish substantive work.

  4. Who performed the substantive portion under the applicable rule

    Correct: verify substantive MDM or more than half of distinct time for eligible noncritical E/M, plus documentation and FS requirements.
