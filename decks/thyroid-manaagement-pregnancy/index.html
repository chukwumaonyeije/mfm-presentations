The foundation

After thyroidectomy, medicine replaces the hormone your body needs

1The thyroid normally makes hormones that regulate energy and support many organs.
2After thyroid removal, the body cannot make enough thyroid hormone on its own.
3Levothyroxine replaces thyroxine, also called T4. It is safe and necessary during pregnancy.
✓

With appropriate thyroid-hormone replacement and follow-up, most patients can expect a healthy pregnancy.

Why monitoring changes

Pregnancy can increase thyroid hormone requirements

Early pregnancy

The fetus initially depends on maternal thyroid hormone. Patients with known hypothyroidism need prompt testing when pregnancy begins.

Mid-pregnancy

Laboratory results and dose changes guide ongoing treatment. Monitoring may become less frequent after levels stabilize.

Late pregnancy

Continue the prescribed dose and complete requested testing. The care team interprets results in the context of pregnancy.

i

A dose that worked before pregnancy may not be the right dose during pregnancy. Do not make a dose change without your clinician’s plan.

Clinical significance

The goal is adequate treatment, not fear

Untreated or inadequately treated overt hypothyroidism is associated with pregnancy complications. Appropriate levothyroxine treatment reduces avoidable risk.

For the pregnant patient

Maintain normal thyroid function

Inadequate treatment has been associated with preeclampsia, anemia, placental problems, and postpartum hemorrhage, particularly when hypothyroidism is severe.

For the developing baby

Support growth and neurodevelopment

Thyroid hormone is crucial for fetal brain and nervous-system development, especially early in pregnancy. Inadequate treatment has also been associated with preterm birth and low birth weight.

Association does not mean every patient will experience a complication. Individual risk depends on laboratory control, history, and the rest of the pregnancy.

Medication routine

Consistency helps levothyroxine work predictably

Use the same routine each day

Take levothyroxine exactly as prescribed. Follow your clinician’s instructions about timing and food.

  • Do not stop or change the dose on your own.
  • Separate levothyroxine from prenatal vitamins containing iron or calcium by at least four hours.
  • Tell the care team about missed doses, vomiting, new medicines, vitamins, or supplements.
  • Complete repeat blood testing after a dose change when requested.

Individualized follow-up

The care plan connects testing, treatment, and specialist follow-up

1

Confirm pregnancy

Contact the care team promptly for thyroid-function testing and dose guidance.

2

During pregnancy

Continue the prescribed medication and complete thyroid testing when requested.

3

After dose changes

Repeat laboratory testing at the interval recommended by the treating clinician.

4

Through delivery

Keep the follow-up schedule designed for the complete pregnancy.

i

Well-controlled hypothyroidism alone does not automatically require weekly fetal surveillance. Follow-up is individualized using the complete maternal and obstetric picture.

When to call

Contact your obstetric team promptly for warning signs

1

Regular contractions

Especially if they are increasing in strength or frequency.

2

Leaking fluid

A gush or ongoing trickle of fluid from the vagina.

3

Vaginal bleeding

Report bleeding according to the urgent plan your team provided.

4

Decreased movement

Call if your baby is moving less than usual or you are concerned.

Use the emergency instructions provided by your obstetric practice. If you believe you are experiencing an emergency, seek emergency care.

After delivery

Thyroid care continues after the baby is born

01

Confirm the dose plan

Pregnancy-related dose needs often fall after delivery. Follow the clinician-directed postpartum prescription.

02

Repeat thyroid testing

Thyroid function is commonly reassessed at about six weeks postpartum, or sooner when clinically indicated.

03

Coordinate follow-up

Endocrinology and obstetric clinicians can adjust the long-term replacement dose from the results.

✓

Levothyroxine is compatible with breastfeeding. Do not stop replacement therapy after delivery unless your clinician directs you to do so.
