01 / Understanding your diagnosis

Polyhydramnios • A guide for you

Extra fluid means we should take a closer look

Polyhydramnios means more fluid than expected around your baby.

Let’s talk about the scan, your next steps, and when to call.

Sources: SMFM [1]; NHS [3] • For pregnancies with one baby.

02 / The fluid around your baby

Fluid helps your baby move and grow

Amniotic fluid is the water-like fluid inside the womb.

It cushions your baby and gives them room to move.

Your baby swallows fluid and passes urine back into it.

Source: SMFM [1] •

06 / Looking for a cause

Your team checks blood sugar and your baby’s growth

Blood sugar test

Diabetes in pregnancy can cause extra fluid. Your team checks your test results and decides if you need another test.

Detailed ultrasound

The scan checks growth and body parts, including parts that help your baby swallow.

A reassuring scan helps, but some swallowing problems are hard to see before birth.

Source: SMFM [1] •

07 / Choosing the next tests

More tests depend on what the first checks show

Reassuring checks

Mild extra fluid alone

A needle test of the fluid, called amniocentesis, is not needed for every patient.

Other concerns or severe fluid

Discuss more testing

Blood tests or tests for genetic conditions may help explain the finding.

Ask what each test could tell you, what its risks are, and how the result would change care.

Source: SMFM [1] •

08 / Follow-up for mild fluid

Mild extra fluid often needs less extra testing

Your next scan

Your team may repeat an ultrasound to check the fluid and your baby’s growth.

Ask when your next visit should be.

Extra heart-rate tests are not needed for mild unexplained fluid alone.

Other health concerns can change this plan.

Sources: SMFM [1]; ACOG [2] •

09 / Follow-up for moderate or severe fluid

More fluid may mean closer checks

Your team may suggest checks of your baby’s heart rate or well-being.

These may include a heart-rate monitor or an ultrasound that checks movement and other signs.

A possible testing plan

32–34

weeks: when checks may start

Often once or twice a week for moderate or severe fluid.

Your start date and schedule depend on your pregnancy. Repeat scans also check fluid and growth.

Source: ACOG [2] • These checks may be considered; the schedule is individual.

10 / Treatment

Treatment depends on the cause and your symptoms

01

If a cause is found

Your team treats that cause, such as high blood sugar.

02

If severe fluid causes major symptoms

A specialist may drain some fluid to ease severe discomfort or trouble breathing.

A medicine called indomethacin should not be used just to lower the fluid level.

Source: SMFM [1] •

11 / Why the care plan matters

Your care team plans for possible problems

Before birth

Extra fluid can raise the chance of early labor or the baby being in a position other than head down.

During and after birth

The team watches for problems when the water breaks, such as the cord slipping ahead of the baby. They also prepare for heavier bleeding after birth.

Rarely, loss of the baby can occur. Your fluid number alone cannot tell us your baby’s risk.

These are possible problems, not predictions. Ask which risks matter most in your pregnancy.

Sources: SMFM [1]; ACOG [2]; NHS [3] •

12 / Planning for birth

Extra fluid does not automatically mean early birth

Mild + no other concerns

Labor can start on its own at term

If labor is started by your team for this reason alone, it should not be before 39 weeks.

Moderate or severe

Make an individual plan

Symptoms, test results, and other findings guide when to give birth.

Severe

Plan the right hospital

Choose a hospital with specialist care for newborn babies.

Vaginal birth may still be possible. Extra fluid alone does not mean you need a cesarean birth (C-section).

Source: SMFM [1] •

13 / When to get help

Call right away for warning signs

Call labor and delivery now

  • Water breaks or fluid leaks
  • Your baby moves less than usual
  • Vaginal bleeding
  • Regular, painful contractions
  • Sudden belly swelling or pain

Severe trouble breathing?

Seek emergency care now. Call your local emergency number.

Do not wait for your next visit.

Sources: NHS [3]; supplied counseling precautions •

14 / Questions to ask your MFM specialist

Ask your MFM specialist for a clear plan

An MFM specialist is a doctor who cares for higher-risk pregnancies.

  1. Is my fluid mild, moderate, or severe?
  2. What tests are done or still needed?
  3. When is my next check?
  4. Does this change my birth plan?
  5. Who do I call after hours?
