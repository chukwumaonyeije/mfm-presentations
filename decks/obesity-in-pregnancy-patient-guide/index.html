OPENMFM / 02

Understanding BMI

BMI is one part of your health picture.

BMI, or body mass index, uses height and weight. Your team uses your weight before pregnancy, or an early-pregnancy estimate.

Class I

30–34.9BMI

Class II

35–39.9BMI

Class III

40+BMI

Class III obesity is sometimes called “morbid obesity.” These terms describe a medical category, not your worth or your whole health.

Your health history matters alongside the number.
OPENMFM / 03

Why follow-up matters

Extra care helps your team watch for problems.

Your health

Checks look for high blood sugar and high blood pressure.

Your baby’s health

Scans and, when needed, extra testing help assess growth and well-being.

Some risks, including stillbirth, rise with higher BMI. Your team uses your full health picture to plan care.

An increased risk does not mean a complication will happen.
OPENMFM / 04

Your first visits

Your health history helps shape the plan.

Tell us about your health

Blood pressure, diabetes, past blood clots, and any weight-loss surgery.

Tell us about sleep and medicines

Loud snoring, pauses in breathing, and all medicines or supplements, including weight-loss medicines.

“What in my history changes the care I need?”
Sleep apnea means breathing repeatedly stops and starts during sleep. Tell your team if you use CPAP, a device that helps keep your airway open.
OPENMFM / 05

Daily habits

Focus on nourishment and steady habits.

A usual weight-gain range

11–20 lbAbout 5–9 kg total for one baby when BMI before pregnancy is 30 or higher.

Make the plan work for you

Choose nourishing meals and movement you can manage. Ask about a dietitian. If safe for you, build toward 150 minutes of moderate activity each week.

Your team can adjust the goal for nutrition and baby’s growth. Do not start a weight-loss diet during pregnancy.
OPENMFM / 06

Diabetes screening

Diabetes checks happen at different times.

Early pregnancy

Testing based on risk can look for diabetes that was already present before pregnancy.

24–28 weeks

Routine screening checks for gestational diabetes: high blood sugar first found during pregnancy.

A normal early result does not replace the usual 24–28 week check. If diabetes is already diagnosed, follow your diabetes care plan.
OPENMFM / 07

Preventing preeclampsia

Ask whether aspirin belongs in your plan.

Preeclampsia is a pregnancy blood-pressure disorder that can affect organs. Low-dose aspirin helps prevent it in some patients.

OPENMFM / 09

Extra baby checks

Extra checks depend on your BMI and health.

These tests check the baby’s heartbeat and sometimes fluid or movement. Weekly testing may be considered:

BMI 40+
34weeks onward
BMI 35–39.9
37weeks onward

Based on BMI before pregnancy. Other conditions may change the schedule. BMI 30–34.9 alone does not set this extra-testing schedule.

Testing helps guide care but cannot prevent every problem. Agree on a schedule with your team.
OPENMFM / 10

When to give birth

Your birth date is a shared decision.

Discuss induction from 39 weeks

Induction means using treatment to start labor. It may be an option, based on your health and preferences.

If pregnancy continues

Agree on follow-up, baby checks, and when to revisit the delivery plan.

BMI alone is not a reason for birth before 39 weeks. A medical complication may require earlier delivery.
OPENMFM / 11

How to give birth

A higher BMI does not automatically mean a cesarean.

“What makes vaginal birth or cesarean the better choice for me?”

Vaginal birth

Can be an option. Your prior births and how labor progresses matter.

Cesarean birth

May be needed for a separate medical reason or a problem during labor.

A prior cesarean needs its own discussion about safe birth options.
OPENMFM / 12

Understanding induction

Induction often starts by preparing the cervix.

The cervix is the opening of the womb.

Ripening means softening and opening it before labor gets going.

01

Prepare

A small balloon, medicine such as misoprostol, or both.

→
02

Reassess

Check the cervix and the baby’s heartbeat.

→
03

Support labor

Oxytocin through an IV may help contractions.

Your team adjusts the steps to your needs.
OPENMFM / 13

What the evidence says

No single induction method is best for everyone.

What we know

A balloon plus medicine can shorten induction in suitable patients overall.

What remains uncertain

Studies have not established one best approach for class III obesity. Larger medicine doses are not automatically better.

Prior cesarean or major womb surgery? Tell your team. Misoprostol is not used for term induction in these situations because it can increase the risk of the womb scar tearing.

OPENMFM / 15

Pain relief

Plan pain relief before labor becomes urgent.

Talk before labor

An anesthesia specialist helps plan pain relief and care if surgery is needed.

Share what matters

Past epidural or anesthesia problems, sleep apnea, and CPAP use.

An epidural uses a small tube in your back to give pain-relief medicine. Placement can take extra time, so discuss when to request it.

Ask about your first choice and a backup plan.

OPENMFM / 17

When to get help

Know the symptoms that need help now.

Call 911 for an emergency

Chest pain, severe trouble breathing, fainting, or heavy bleeding with weakness or dizziness.

Get medical care right away

Severe headache, vision changes, one-sided leg pain or swelling, or heavy bleeding. Call maternity care right away if baby moves less than usual.

Do not wait for the next visit. If you cannot reach your team, go for urgent assessment. These warning signs matter during pregnancy and after birth.
