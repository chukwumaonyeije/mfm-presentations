# Pregnancy with a Higher BMI
## Your care, your choices, your birth plan

OpenMFM Presentation Standard v1.0 · Outline for approval · November 4, 2026

Requested topic: Morbid obesity during pregnancy. Audience: patients with prepregnancy BMI above 30, with clearly marked additional considerations for BMI 40 or higher.

### Educational design

- **Central message:** Your BMI helps your team plan care; your health, your baby's health, and your preferences shape the decisions.
- **Language:** Use respectful, person-first language. Explain that class III obesity (BMI ≥40) is the medical category sometimes called “morbid obesity.” Do not apply that category to everyone with BMI above 30.
- **Prerequisites:** None. Define BMI, preeclampsia, induction, cervical ripening, and anesthesia when first introduced.
- **Misconceptions to address:** A higher BMI guarantees complications; pregnancy requires weight loss; everyone needs the same extra testing; cesarean is automatic; one induction method is best for everyone.
- **Decisions:** Nutrition and movement goals, diabetes screening, aspirin eligibility, extra fetal checks, delivery timing, induction options, pain relief, and postpartum follow-up.
- **Six-month takeaway:** Keep a personal care plan, know when to seek help, and ask how each test or treatment changes care.
- **Scope:** Primarily US guidance, singleton pregnancy. Twins, prior uterine surgery, and other medical conditions require different plans.
- **Design:** Dark navy, cyan accents, generous spacing, one major idea per slide. Use care timelines and simple diagrams instead of dense risk tables or body-size imagery. Aim for accessible patient vocabulary.

### Evidence decisions before HTML production

1. **Update the early-screening language.** The source packet mixes early screening for existing diabetes with routine early gestational diabetes screening. Use the 2024 ACOG update for this distinction; verify its primary full text before final slide wording.
2. **Retain conditional surveillance language.** Use “may be considered,” with shared decisions. Do not present testing as mandatory for all BMI categories or as eliminating stillbirth risk.
3. **Avoid a universal delivery deadline.** Do not carry over “do not go past 40–41 weeks” as a universal ACOG rule. Discuss individualized timing and reassessment.
4. **Do not call combination induction best-in-class.** Explain that it is an option; obesity-specific studies have limitations. The 2024 dosing trial did not demonstrate an overall reduction in induction duration with the higher dose; it does not justify patient-directed dose escalation.
5. **Omit unverified numerical claims.** The supplied unnamed 2026 balloon cohort and the 57% versus 39% comparison lack complete traceable citations. They are unnecessary for this patient narrative and will not be presented as established benefits.
6. **No drug dosing protocols.** Omit misoprostol, oxytocin, enoxaparin, antibiotic, and high-dose folic-acid regimens. Explicitly flag prior cesarean as relevant to induction medication safety.
7. **No implied endorsement or license.** Do not reproduce the source packet's “used under license” statement. Write original patient explanations and cite sources.
