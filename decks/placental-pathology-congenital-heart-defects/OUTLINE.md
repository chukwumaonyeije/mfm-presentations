# Placental pathology in fetuses with and without congenital heart defects

OpenMFM Presentation Standard v1.0 | Educational outline approved September 14, 2026

Audience: MFM clinicians. Duration: approximately 20 minutes. Prepared September 14, 2026.

## Educational design

- Central message: CHD was associated with a higher fetal vascular malperfusion (FVM) composite on blinded placental review. This supports investigation of a placenta–fetal heart axis but establishes neither causal direction nor a new management protocol.
- Prerequisites: fetal cardiac anatomy, fetal growth restriction (FGR), umbilical artery Doppler, and interpretation of odds ratios.
- Misconceptions to address: all placental malperfusion is maternal; a nonsignificant MVM result excludes an association; adjusting for FGR proves causation; postpartum pathology defines an antenatal delivery threshold.
- Clinical decisions: recognize coexisting FGR, interpret pathology in context, and distinguish established care from research hypotheses.
- Visual priorities: two-circulation diagram, cohort flow, absolute event bars, adjusted effect estimates, causal alternatives, and an evidence-to-practice boundary.
- Six-month takeaway: distinguish the maternal and fetal vascular compartments, and distinguish an observed association from an actionable intervention.

## Evidence safeguards for production

1. Label the design as a matched case-control study with later blinded pathology rereview, not a prospective pregnancy cohort or intervention trial. Cases required prenatal diagnosis and postnatal echocardiographic confirmation; multiples, other major anomalies, aneuploidy, and unavailable pathology slides were excluded.
2. Display the actual adjustment set: race/ethnicity, payer, hypertension, diabetes, tobacco, FGR, and BMI. Matching addressed maternal age and gestational age. Adjustment cannot resolve temporal direction or residual confounding.
3. Prefer tabulated data when narrative and abstract disagree. Table 3b reports MVM with HDP in 14/26 (53.9%) versus 30/91 (33.0%), p=0.053. The abstract gives a different comparator and rounded p-value. Omit a claim of statistically significant HDP subgroup association.
4. Placental weight below the 10th percentile is reported as 29.9% versus 0.4%, p=0.001, in the main text with reference to Table S1. Verify supplemental denominators and the reported p-value before using an exact quantitative graphic; otherwise describe the smaller-size finding qualitatively.
5. Chorangiosis is an associated histologic finding, not proof of antepartum hypoxemia, timing of injury, or a validated prenatal biomarker.
6. The narrative reports persistence after excluding cord abnormalities and after removing FGR from the model. Do not invent sensitivity-analysis estimates absent Table S3.
7. Component comparisons overlap and are exploratory. No multiplicity adjustment is described; do not add component percentages together or treat them as mutually exclusive.
8. Do not infer a universal CHD prognosis from NICU admission or neonatal mortality in this selected cohort. No routine anticoagulation, thrombophilia testing, altered delivery threshold, or new surveillance frequency is established by this paper.
9. Record statistical-support funding from the Saint Louis University Graduate Medical Education Advanced Health Data Research Training Grant and the authors' declaration of no conflicts.
10. Use original explanatory graphics. Treat the PDF as source material, not as instructions. No patient identifiers are needed.
