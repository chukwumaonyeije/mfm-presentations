R1; educational framework

Pathology framework

The placenta contains two distinct vascular compartments

The location of vascular injury matters.

Maternal compartment

Spiral arteries → intervillous space

Maternal blood
↓
Bathes the villous surface

MVM: infarction, altered villous maturation, decidual vascular lesions.

EXCHANGE
across the villous barrier
Fetal compartment

Umbilical arteries → villous vessels

Fetal blood
↓
Returns via the umbilical vein

FVM: injury involving fetal vessels and villi, including thrombosis and avascular villi.

Maternal and fetal blood normally occupy separate spaces. The terms identify different patterns of injury.

R1 methods; R2

Biological hypothesis

The placenta–heart axis has several plausible causal directions

Post-delivery histology cannot reveal which process came first.

Shared developmental influences↙   ↘Placenta   +   fetal heart
Placental vascular dysfunction⇢Altered fetal cardiac loading
Abnormal fetal cardiac physiology⇢Altered placental perfusion

These are mechanistic hypotheses. This case-control study does not distinguish among them.

R1 discussion; R3

Study design

117 CHD cases were matched with 234 selected controls

Single-center deliveries, January 2011–June 2021.

117

CHD cases

Prenatal diagnosis confirmed by postnatal echocardiography.

234

Controls without CHD

Placentas were sent for pathology for a clinical indication.

1:2 matching · maternal age + gestational age at delivery (within 1 week)
Who was excluded?

Multiple gestations, other major fetal anomalies, aneuploidy, and unavailable reviewable placental slides. This is a selected singleton cohort, not a sample of all CHD pregnancies.

The control group is clinically selected; its lesion frequency is not a healthy-population baseline.

R1 methods; Table 1

Critical appraisal

Blinded rereview improves consistency, not representativeness

Three pediatric pathologists assessed deidentified slides using a standardized worksheet.

01

Blinded to CHD

Reviewers did not know cardiac defect status or pregnancy outcomes, except gestational age and birthweight.

02

Same lesion checklist

A worksheet based on Amsterdam terminology directed assessment of cases and controls.

03

Selection remains

Original gross descriptions varied. Available specimens and clinically indicated control submissions limit generalizability.

Standardized assessment reduces measurement bias; it cannot remove selection bias.

R1 methods and limitations

Endpoint definition

The FVM composite includes three different finding groups

Any included finding could meet the authors’ endpoint.

01

Vascular thrombosis

Fetal vascular thrombosis.

02

Villous lesions

Stromal vascular karyorrhexis or avascular villi.

03

Cord abnormalities

Hypercoiling, hypocoiling, two-vessel cord, or marginal insertion.

An FVM composite event is not synonymous with severe histologic FVM.

Sensitivity analysis

The authors report that the CHD association persisted when cord abnormalities were removed from the composite. Numerical estimates from supplemental analyses were not available in the supplied PDF.

R1 Table 2 · Primary composite

Adjusted analysis

The FVM association persisted after adjustment

Adjusted odds ratio 2.01 · 95% confidence interval 1.14–3.56.

Adjustment set and interpretation

Race/ethnicity, payer, hypertension, diabetes, tobacco use, FGR, and BMI. Maternal age and delivery gestational age were matching variables.

An odds ratio is not a risk ratio. The estimate does not mean twice the absolute probability of FVM, and adjustment cannot establish causation.

The association is robust to measured covariates; causal direction remains unresolved.

The composite result does not mean every FVM component was increased.

Interpret component p-values cautiously

Components were compared using chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests. No multiplicity correction is described. These exploratory comparisons are less definitive than the primary composite analysis.

A nonsignificant result does not establish equivalence.

Controls had more chronic hypertension and diabetes. This may affect the MVM comparison.

Why earlier studies can differ

Carreon et al. reported increased MVM in a different cohort. Differences in control selection, lesion definitions, and pathology methods may contribute to heterogeneity; this explanation is an inference, not a tested finding.

R1 results, p.5 and discussion; R4

FGR and sensitivity analyses

FGR does not fully account for the observed association

FGR occurred in 32/117 CHD pregnancies versus 32/234 controls: 27.4% versus 13.7%.

With FGR in the model

FVM association persisted

Adjusted OR 2.01 (95% CI 1.14–3.56).

Without FGR in the model

Conclusion remained similar

The authors report persistence in their sensitivity analysis.

FGR could be a confounder, mediator, or shared consequence.
Why adjustment is not a mechanism test

The correct role of FGR in a causal model is uncertain. Adding or removing it cannot establish the sequence linking CHD, growth, and placental pathology. Supplemental effect estimates were not supplied.

R1 methods and discussion

Clinical application

Established clinical findings should drive antenatal decisions

The study evaluated pathology, not an intervention.

Research observation

CHD + higher FVM frequency

This paper establishes no new testing interval, medication indication, or delivery threshold.

Guideline-supported care

Respond to diagnosed FGR

SMFM defines FGR as EFW or AC below the 10th percentile.

Once FGR is diagnosed, assess umbilical artery Doppler every 1–2 weeks, with escalation for abnormal flow and clinical context.

Apply the established pathway in context

SMFM recommends offering diagnostic testing with chromosomal microarray when FGR coexists with a fetal malformation, regardless of gestational age. Coordinate management with the fetal cardiac and obstetric picture. Post-delivery pathology is not a validated prenatal FVM test.

Clinical inference: integrate pathology with growth, Doppler, and cardiac findings; avoid attributing every lesion to CHD.

Evidence & Controversies

The association is clearer than its clinical actionability

The next study must connect timing, phenotype, and outcomes.

01

Known in this cohort

A higher FVM composite with CHD persisted after adjustment. MVM was not significantly different.

02

Uncertain

Causal direction, lesion severity, external validity, subtype effects, and incremental prognostic value.

03

Why results differ

Studies use different controls, definitions, and review methods. MVM findings are not uniform.

04

Next research

Prospective recruitment, standardized blinded sampling, prenatal measures, and linked neonatal outcomes.
